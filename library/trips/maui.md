# Maui

The first trip section. A kit list and a storage/battery plan, then
activity-by-activity notes that link back to the general pages.

## Kit list
- X5 camera, Invisible Selfie Stick (and the extended one if you're bringing
  it), Invisible Dive Case Pro (the glass-dome version — clearer than the
  standard plastic dome, for roughly $20 more)
- Electric Suction Cup Mount — for any car/boat exterior shots on the Road
  to Hana or a boat trip; see [Accessories that unlock shots](../gear/accessories-for-shots.md)
- A ground stake (¼″ spike) if you want legless tiny planets on the beach
  or golf course rough
- Optionally, a Dive Buddy (buoyancy handle + seabed stand) if you want any
  hands-free seabed shots while diving
- 2 batteries currently owned, plus a spare battery or a power bank (see
  battery plan below)
- 1×256GB card currently owned, plus **two 1TB cards** (recommended — see
  below)
- USB-C card reader
- Optionally: a portable SSD, for backing up without a laptop
- Mini tripod or the stick's own stand
- Lens cap — the lenses stick out and scratch easily
- Cleaning cloth — wipe both lenses before anything that matters
- Wrist strap (for the helicopter flight and anything over water)

Before the first dive: save a **5.7K60 preset to the Q/power button** (Dive
Case Mode locks most settings once sealed, so set this ahead of time), and
check the camera's **all-weather USB-C door** is seated properly if
charging in humid conditions.

## Storage and battery plan (17 days, no laptop)
You already own one 256GB card and two batteries. For 17 days without a
laptop to offload footage:

- **Buy two 1TB cards** in addition to what you have. That gives headroom
  to shoot for several days at a time before you need to back up, without
  juggling a nearly-full 256GB card daily.
- **Back up without a laptop:** phone + USB-C card reader + portable SSD.
  Copy each card to the SSD every couple of days, confirm the copy, then
  reuse the card. See [memory cards](../gear/memory-cards.md) for the full
  method.
- **Bring a third battery, or a power bank** — three activity-heavy days in
  a row (a dive, a round of golf, an evening event) will outrun two
  batteries without a way to top up mid-day.
- Measure your own real GB/hour from the first day or two of shooting and
  use that to sanity-check whether two 1TB cards is actually enough for the
  rest of the trip — see [storage and batteries](../gear/storage-and-batteries.md)
  for the full method and a worked example.
- **Download every night** rather than letting several days pile up — one
  creator's top travel-tip lesson learned the hard way; it also means a
  lost or damaged card only costs you one day, not the whole trip.

## Activities

### Diving (3 dives)
- **Settings:** see [underwater diving](../settings/underwater-diving.md) —
  Dive Case Mode on, 5.7K30, AquaVision to fix colour afterwards.
- **Shooting approach:** let it roll for the whole dive rather than
  starting and stopping — that's how you catch anything unexpected in the
  water. Check your battery is fresh before the dive: **you can't swap the
  battery inside the dive case**, so one charge has to last the whole dive.
- **Edit approach:** review with the marks/AI Highlights workflow (see
  [finding moments in long clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md)),
  then Deep Track on anything worth following (a diver, a fish) rather than
  Auto Frame.

### Golf (3 rounds — film Kapalua most)
- **Settings:** standard Video, 5.7K30, Auto everything — nothing special
  needed for daylight outdoor golf. See [bright sun and snow](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md)
  if it's a particularly bright day and you want the ND filter/EV notes.
- **Shooting approach:** stick planted or handheld at the tee, let it roll
  through the swing and the walk down the fairway. Kapalua is the round
  worth filming the most — plan to shoot more there and be more selective
  on the other two.
- **Edit approach:** [split and fixed views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md)
  works well for golf — a fixed view on the swing, a short second piece for
  the reaction/view. [Keyframes with easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md)
  if you want a deliberate reveal of the view from a tee box.

### Snorkelling and beach days
- **Settings:** see [snorkelling and water](../settings/snorkelling-and-water.md) —
  no dive case needed, Auto exposure, AquaVision afterwards.
- **Shooting approach:** just let it run — this is the one water scenario
  where you don't need to aim at anything. Swim with it, dunk it, spin it.
- **Edit approach:** mostly triage — skim for the good stretches, Deep
  Track only on anything specific you want followed (a swimmer, a fish),
  otherwise a fixed forward view is enough.

### Luau (night/event)
- **Settings:** see [events and indoors](../settings/events-and-indoors.md) —
  PureVideo for the evening light, manual white balance around 3000–4000K
  for warm venue lighting, or Auto if the light is mixed.
- **Shooting approach:** plant the stick rather than handhold through the
  whole evening — it's less tiring and gives Deep Track a clean static
  frame to work with. Say "mark that" for anything you specifically want to
  find again later.
- **Edit approach:** [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) on whoever's
  active on stage, or [multi-view](../editing/multi-view.md) if you want
  the performance and the table both visible.

### Road to Hana
- **Settings:** TimeShift for the drive itself — see
  [timelapse and TimeShift](../settings/timelapse-and-timeshift.md). Switch
  to normal Video settings for stops.
- **Shooting approach:** mount for the drive, TimeShift running for the
  long stretches of road; stop TimeShift and shoot normally at waterfalls,
  viewpoints, and other stops — don't try to make one continuous clip cover
  both driving and stopping.
- **Edit approach:** TimeShift needs no reframing at all — trim only. Stops
  get the normal ladder — start with
  [fixed views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md) or
  [keyframes](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md) for a reveal shot at a
  viewpoint.

### Helicopter flight
- **Settings:** standard Video, 5.7K30 or 8K if the operator allows it and
  card space is available (see [storage and batteries](../gear/storage-and-batteries.md)).
- **Shooting approach:** **ask the operator about camera rules before the
  flight** — some tour operators restrict loose items, mounts, or where a
  camera can be held. If you're allowed to hold it, keep a wrist strap on
  at all times.
- **Edit approach:** a fixed view or a single long pan usually works best
  for scenic aerial footage — see [keyframes with easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md)
  for a slow reveal move, or just export a fixed forward/downward view for
  something quick.
