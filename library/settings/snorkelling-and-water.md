# Snorkelling and surface water

For casual water use — snorkelling, beach days, kayaking, pool — where you
don't need the dive case. See [Diving](underwater-diving.md) for the case
setup.

## Settings

|---|---|

Save these as your **SNORKEL** preset, with the before-and-after checklist:
see [Quick set-ups](quick-setups.md#3-snorkel-bare-camera-no-case).

## Tips
- The X5 is rated to 15 m (49 ft) with no case, well past snorkelling
  depth. **Lens guards off** first: they aren't waterproof. Check the battery
  and USB doors show **no orange**.
- **Trade-off:** Insta360 says the bare camera underwater may show
  stitching problems in 360, so it recommends the dive case for real
  underwater filming. For in-and-out snorkelling, the bare camera still
  wins on the surface. Keep fish in front of a lens, not at the sides.
- **After salt water:** rinse with the doors closed, soak 5–10 minutes in
  fresh water, then dry before opening anything.
- This is one water scenario where "just let it run" genuinely works —
  don't waste effort trying to aim the camera at fish or a moving subject.
  Swim with it, dunk it under, spin it around; frame it later.
- A short stick or wrist strap is enough at the surface. Save the dive case
  for real depth.
- See [Underwater diving](underwater-diving.md) for dive-case handling
  notes (framing toward the sun, minimising rotation, AquaVision 3.0) that
  also apply if you use the case in a pool or for deeper snorkelling.

## More tips
See [Spray-Proof Lens & Leash](../effects/spray-proof-lens-and-leash.md) for
the full page. Highlights: an old diver's **spray-off trick** — lick the
lens, then rinse and dry, so spray runs off instead of beading; **sunscreen
on your hands ruins a lens faster than water does** — rub it on your
wetsuit shoulder and dunk it rather than wiping with a finger; and
**check the battery/USB doors are fully closed every time** — a
millimetre open is enough to let water in.
