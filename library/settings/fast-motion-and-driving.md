# Fast motion and driving

Covers cars, mountain biking, and helmet-mounted sports. Full 360 is still
the default — see the note on Single-Lens Mode below for the one real
exception.

## Settings
| | Driving | Mountain biking (full 360) | Motorsport / helmet POV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mode | Video, or Road Mode; PureVideo at dusk/night | Video, full 360 | **Single-Lens Mode** |
| Res/FPS | 5.7K30 | 5.7K30 or 4K60 | Up to 4K60 |
| EV | Auto | Auto, Active HDR on for dappled shade | Auto |
| White balance | Auto | Auto | Auto |
| Stabilisation | FlowState on | FlowState + Horizon Lock | N/A |

## Tips
- 5.7K30 gives better low-light/dynamic range for glare and tunnels than
  8K, and keeps file sizes down on long drives.
- Mount so one lens points along the direction of travel — forward through
  the windscreen, one back into the cabin for a car.
- For mountain biking, a chest mount gives a steadier horizon than a
  helmet, since it tips less with head movement.
- **Speed-blur shutter trick:** in lower light (dusk, forest shade), a
  manual shutter of 1/60–1/120 lets the frame edges blur while you stay
  sharp, making riding or driving read as much faster. In daylight, get
  the same effect with an ND filter instead of underexposing. Not a good
  choice for shots with kids' faces close to the lens — the blur reads as
  motion sickness rather than speed there.
- **ND "shutter = half the interval" rule** (for timelapses at speed):
  set the shutter to roughly half your interval length and add an ND
  filter — this is also the anti-flicker fix for LCD/streetlight strobing.

!!! tip "The one exception to 'always full 360'"
    Helmet-POV motorsport (motorcycle, go-kart) is the one case where
    Single-Lens Mode genuinely makes sense: at speed there's no reframing
    time to spend, no stitch line to fight, and the other lens would only
    show your own helmet or bike. This is a niche exception — don't use
    Single-Lens Mode for general shooting.
