# Timelapse and TimeShift

Two different tools for two different situations — pick based on whether
**you're** moving, not the subject.

## Settings
| | TimeShift (you're moving) | Timelapse/Interval (camera is static) | Night timelapse / stars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mode | TimeShift | Timelapse (continuous) or Interval (fixed gaps) | Timelapse, or Photo mode's Interval/long exposure |
| Speed/shutter | Auto, or manual up to 60x | Manual shutter as slow as 1 second for a light-trail look | Shutter up to 1 second (Timelapse) or 2 minutes (Photo mode) |
| Exposure | Auto | Manual if the scene spans a big light change (sunset to night) | Manual — go fully manual for this one |
| ISO | Auto | Low, manual | Reportedly around ISO 800–1600, test and adjust |
| White balance | Auto | Auto | Manual, fixed around 4000K (Auto can flicker between frames) |

## Tips
- **TimeShift** is for when you're walking, driving, or on a lift and want a
  sped-up "flying through the scene" clip. It renders already-sped-up and
  flat, in-camera, at roughly a tenth the file size of normal video, with
  zero reframing needed after.
- **Timelapse/Interval** is for a static camera watching the scene change —
  clouds, day into night, a busy street emptying and filling.
- A tripod is mandatory for anything static — camera movement during a
  multi-minute-to-multi-hour timelapse ruins it. A power bank helps for long
  sessions.
- Star shots need the camera completely still and fully manual settings —
  this is the one scenario on the X5 where Auto genuinely isn't the answer.
- Don't try TimeShift for a static scene or Timelapse mode while moving —
  match the mode to whether you're the one moving.
