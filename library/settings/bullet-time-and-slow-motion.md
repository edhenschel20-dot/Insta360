# Bullet time and slow motion

Both need a high frame rate, and both trade resolution for it.

## Settings
| | Bullet time | Slow motion |
|---|---|---|
| Mode | Dedicated Bullet Time mode | Standard Video at high fps (Single-Lens if you specifically want max fps) |
| Res/FPS | Up to 5.7K120 (check what's on offer in-app — this varies by firmware) | 4K100/120 as the general choice; 2.7K120 only if you need more slow-down |
| Exposure | Auto — the two lenses need to agree as you swing between them. Use **Balanced Exposure** if chest-mounted | Auto normally; bias shutter faster than usual if shooting manual, so frames stay crisp once slowed |
| Light | Needs good light — low light forces a slower shutter, which shows as motion blur on a fast swing | Avoid low light for the same reason |

## Tips
- Swing the stick at roughly one rotation per second, keeping one lens up
  and one down as you swing.
- Bullet Time is a ready-to-share clip straight off the camera — no
  reframing needed, just trim the lead-in/lead-out.
- Higher fps forces lower resolution on the X5 — that's a hardware limit,
  not a setting to fight. 4K120 is the general-purpose choice for slow-mo.
- Don't expect 8K quality at 120fps.
