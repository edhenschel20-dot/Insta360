# Speed-Blur Shutter Trick
A manual, slower shutter speed streaks the edges of the frame while you stay sharp, so a ride, drive, or golf-cart trip feels much faster than it actually was.

**Difficulty:** Medium (needs manual settings, not a one-tap effect) · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Best in lower light (dusk, forest, overcast) with a manual shutter of roughly 1/60–1/120.
2. In daylight, add an ND filter so you can keep the same slow shutter without overexposing — see [Bright sun & snow settings](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md) for which ND to reach for and the 180°-shutter rule.
3. Ride, drive, or cart normally at a decent pace — the blur does the rest.

## Edit it (phone app)
No special edit needed — the blur is baked in at shoot time. Reframe as you normally would.

## Settings
- Manual shutter 1/60–1/120; ND filter in daylight.
- Not recommended for shots with kids' faces close to the lens — the same blur makes faces harder to make out.

## Tips
- This is a settings trick, not a mode — you have to switch to manual before you start, so it suits a planned shot (a fairway golf-cart ride, a bike descent) rather than "just let it roll" footage.
- See also [Ride / Run Stats Overlay](stats-overlay.md) to add real speed numbers to the same clip.
