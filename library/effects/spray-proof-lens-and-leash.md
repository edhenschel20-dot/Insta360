# Spray-Proof Lens & Leash
Three small habits that matter more than any setting when water's involved: keeping spray off the lenses, keeping the camera physically attached to something, and checking the ports are actually sealed.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. **The lens trick:** an old diving trick — lick the lens, then rinse and dry it. The thin film left behind makes spray run off instead of beading up and blurring the shot. Do this before you start, not mid-session.
2. **Leash everything:** if the X5 is in its cage, thread twine or a proper leash through the cage and tie it to something solid — a mast, a rail, a boat cleat.
3. **Check the doors:** double-check the battery and USB doors are fully closed every single time you use it near water. A millimetre open is enough to let water in.
4. **Quick capture:** set this up so one tap starts and stops recording — you don't want to be fumbling with menus with wet hands.
5. **Surf/board version:** rub the lens on your wetsuit shoulder and dunk it before every wave, rather than wiping with a finger — sunscreen on a finger smears worse than the water itself.
6. **Boat version:** leash the camera to the rail, and take it out of any housing between sightings if the housing risks reflecting the white camera body inside it (see the boat-based pages for the specific reflection warning).

## Tips
- The "lick and rinse" trick sounds odd but is a genuine diver's habit for exactly this problem — do it once before you start rather than trying to wipe spray off mid-session.
- Wind noise on an open boat or board is a separate problem — see the Utility Frame accessory (covered elsewhere in this library) if wind is the bigger issue than spray.
- Pairs with any of the water pages in this library — worth doing regardless of which specific water shot you're after that day.
