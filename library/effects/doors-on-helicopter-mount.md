# Doors-On Helicopter: Lens-on-Glass & Cabin Suction Mount
Through-window aerial views without reflections of your own clothing, plus the whole cabin — family reactions included — from one camera.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Reflections come from the cabin, not the window — get the near lens as close to the glass as the operator allows, and wear dark clothing. A lens hood (the normal photographer's fix) isn't possible on a 360 camera, so lens-to-glass is the substitute; the far lens gets the cabin, which is fine.
2. **Tether everything.** Agree with the pilot beforehand where a camera may go, and never rely on suction mounts outside the aircraft — pressure changes can pull them off.
3. Inside: a suction mount on the canopy or window can see the whole cabin at once; a stick or a folding grip held at the window works for a standard tour flight.
4. A wireless mic in a headset ear-cup gives clean intercom audio instead of engine noise.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. Fixed view out of the window, with a slow zoom-out as the view opens up — see [Aircraft / Gondola Window Shot](aircraft-gondola-window-shot.md) for that technique in detail.
2. [Split-Screen MultiView](split-screen-multiview.md): cabin reactions plus the view outside.

## Settings
Full reference: [Bright sun and snow](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md) — similar bright, high-contrast light at altitude.
- 5.7K60 for banking turns.

## Tips
- This is entirely down to the operator's rules on a doors-on flight — ask before assuming anything can be mounted anywhere.
- Plan the mount position with the pilot before takeoff, not once you're airborne.
