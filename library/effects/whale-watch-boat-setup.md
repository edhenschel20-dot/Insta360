# Whale-Watch Boat Setup
A breach and the kids' reaction in the same clip, filmed from a moving boat, without chasing the whale around with the camera.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Stand at the back of the boat — the most stable spot — and lean your hips on the rail.
2. 60fps for most of the trip; only switch to 120fps right as a breach happens, since a whale just surfacing at 120fps is 50 seconds of nothing when you play it back.
3. Don't move the camera to chase the whale — anticipate where it will come up and let the 360 have it. Pick a calm day if you get a choice of trips.
4. Keep it rolling the whole time out, not just when a whale is visible — they surface behind you as often as in front.
5. Lens toward the water on whichever side the captain calls.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. [Split-Screen MultiView](split-screen-multiview.md): the whale on one side, the kids' faces on the other — the point of a 360 camera here is that you don't have to choose between them.
2. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) the whale only after it actually surfaces, not while you're waiting.

## Tips
- If you're using the dive housing on deck, the white X5 body can reflect inside it — swap to the black body, or leave the housing off while you're just up on deck.
- Don't turn the camera to follow the whale the way you would with a phone — that's the one habit worth unlearning here.
