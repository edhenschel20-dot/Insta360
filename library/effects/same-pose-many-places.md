# Same Pose, Many Places (Spin Me Around)
The "same person, same pose, several locations" effect — you spin on the stick at each place and the app tornado-cuts between them. There's a one-tap template for this, plus manual versions with more control over speed and framing.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Extend the stick fully and hold it level, arms/elbows locked straight.
2. At each location, rotate yourself slowly on the spot, **always in the same direction**, for at least 25 seconds (one creator goes a full minute so the app can reliably find the clip).
3. Keep the same stick length and roughly the same height at every location — that consistency is what makes the cut believable.
4. Repeat for 3–4 locations.

## Edit it (phone app)
**One-tap route (template):**
1. Edit › Shot Lab › **Spin Me Around** › Use this theme.
2. Pick the clips you shot.
3. Reorder them by press-and-hold if needed.
4. Export — the app adds the motion blur automatically.

**Manual route (more control over speed/centring):**
1. Keep yourself centred in every clip — either [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md), or one keyframe per quarter-turn with your head on the same grid line each time.
2. Speed-ramp the last half of clip A and the first half of clip B up to roughly 8×, with Motion ND on to blur the spin.
3. Cross-dissolve on the fastest part of the ramp, where neither background is recognisable.
4. This same shoot works for two named variants creators use: "Landscape Switch" (Gimbal Guru) and "Tornado Transition" (Ben Claremont) — they're the same technique with different names.

## Adapt it
This one is already hands-free-friendly (you're the one spinning, not a vehicle), so it needs no motorcycle-style adaptation — just keep the kids' version simple: a slow, steady spin works better with children than a fast one.

## Settings
- 5.7K30 is enough; Motion ND helps sell the spin blur on both the template and manual routes.

## Tips
- Try **Spin Me Around** first — it's genuinely one of the easier Shot Lab templates to get right (Ben Claremont ranks it S-tier, "really easy to film and edit").
- Related but different: **Time Flip** and **Flip My Day** spin 180° at each spot instead of a full rotation and stitch the halves — see [Time Flip & Flip My Day](time-flip-flip-my-day.md). They need more genuinely different-looking backgrounds and are more fiddly to plan (Ben rates them D-tier).
- If you want individual stills spun together instead of clips of yourself, that's a different technique — see [Spin One Photo Into Another](spin-photo-into-photo.md).
