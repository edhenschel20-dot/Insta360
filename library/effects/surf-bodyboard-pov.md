# Surf & Bodyboard POV (Bite Mount)
Point-of-view from inside the wave, hands completely free on the board, with the X5 held in a bite mount.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Fit the X5 into the Insta360 bite mount — see [Mouth-Mount / Bite POV](mouth-mount-bite-pov.md) for the general mounting technique. In a pinch it works held bare in the teeth, but every exhale fogs the lens, so use the mount and breathe through your nose instead.
2. Turn on screen lock so water drops don't change settings mid-session, and use quick-capture so one tap starts and stops.
3. Press record as the wave appears, not while you're still paddling for it.
4. Angle rule: tilt up for barrels and whitewater (shows the lip), tilt down to show the board and your feet for turns.
5. Leash the mount to your wetsuit cord.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. Reframe to the angle that best matches what was actually happening — barrel (up) or turn (down) — rather than one fixed angle for the whole ride.
2. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) the wave face, or export "one clip, two formats" for both a wide reef view and a close POV.

## Settings
Full reference: [Snorkelling & water settings](../settings/snorkelling-and-water.md).
- 5.7K60 so the wave can be slowed in the edit if you want it.

## Tips
- Never wipe the lens with a finger — sunscreen mist smears worse than water. Rub it on your wetsuit shoulder and dunk instead.
- For kids bodyboarding in whitewater, the same bite mount works, or a floaty handle if a mouth mount isn't practical for them.
- An "invisible stick" shot — the stick trailing behind you, out of frame — is worth trying alongside the bite-mount POV on the same session.
