# Location Swap (Wall Slide Transition)
You hold one pose against a wall while the camera slides sideways past you (right to left); do the same pose and slide at several different walls, then cut the clips together at the matching moment so it looks like you never moved but the background did. Creators also call this a "wrap-around transition", "wall slide" or "teleport transition".

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Works on X5:** Yes

This demo is a regular (non-360) phone/gimbal version — the technique is identical on the X5, just done in 360 with a reframe afterward instead of a physical straight-line walk. No 360-specific tutorial of this exact trick has turned up yet; this is the closest verified match.

## Shoot it
1. Pick a wall or backdrop and a pose you can hold rock-still: arms folded, hands in pockets, a peace sign — whatever's easy to repeat exactly.
2. Keep the same distance from you to the wall and the same camera height at every location — pace out a few steps, or use a floor tile/paving line as a marker so it's repeatable.
3. Slide the camera sideways past you, **always in the same direction** (e.g. right to left) and at roughly the same speed each time. On the X5 you can do this by physically walking or sliding the stick sideways — the 360 capture means you don't have to nail the framing at shoot time, you fix that in the reframe.
4. Start and end each take with something covering the lens if you can — a wall edge, a post, a doorway — that gives you a clean, matching frame to cut on. If nothing's available, just make sure there's a moment in each clip where you're dead centre in frame.
5. Repeat the whole thing at 6–7 different walls or backdrops, keeping pose, height, distance, direction and speed the same every time.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. In each clip's Keyframe editor, reframe (grid on) so you land in the same spot on screen — ideally at the exact moment you're centred and the slide is mid-blur. Keep your head on the same grid line in every clip; that consistency is what sells the cut.
2. Trim each clip to that matching moment and butt the next clip on directly — this is a hard cut/match cut, no transition effect needed.
3. To hide the join further, add a short speed ramp (up to ~4–8×) with Motion ND over the last/first half-second either side of the cut, so the background is a blur right at the cut point — same approach as the speed ramp in [Same Pose, Many Places](same-pose-many-places.md).
4. If you used the "something covers the lens" method: trim each clip so it starts and ends at the moment the object fully fills the frame, then cut wall-covered-to-wall-covered. That's the manual (non-360) version shown in the video above, and it works just as well on flattened X5 footage.

## Adapt it
- **Kids version:** have him strike the same silly pose (superhero stance, peace sign, arms crossed) against 6–7 different walls around town or on holiday. He doesn't need to be perfectly still — a rough hold is enough, since the changing wall behind him is what sells the trick — and repeating the same pose over and over is usually funny to him in itself.
- **Walking version:** if standing still gets boring for a group, everyone can take a couple of small steps in place instead of holding a statue-still pose — as long as the overall silhouette and position in frame stays close, the cut still reads.

## Settings
- Any resolution you already shoot at. Motion ND helps if you're adding a speed ramp over the cut, same as the spinning version.

## Tips
- This is the straight-line cousin of [Same Pose, Many Places (Spin Me Around)](same-pose-many-places.md): same idea — you stay put, the place around you changes — but here the camera slides sideways past you instead of you spinning on the stick. Try Spin Me Around first if you want the one-tap Shot Lab route; use this one when you'd rather keep the reframe fully manual or don't want to spin on the spot with kids.
- Plan your 7 locations before you set off (a mental "wall crawl" on a walk or day out) — hunting for a matching wall on the day slows things down.
- A doorway, gatepost or tree trunk at the start/end of the slide gives you a natural "wipe" to cut on, same as the [Pass-By Wipe](pass-by-wipe.md).
