# Tiny Planet Photo Composition
Five composition rules that separate a tiny planet photo that reads well from one that just looks like a smudged ball — for use alongside the base [Tiny Planet & Inverted Tiny Planet](tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) technique.

**Difficulty:** Medium (composition takes practice; the reframe step itself is easy) · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. **Turn your back on the view.** A landmark ends up on top of the planet when it's behind you, not in front of you.
2. **Find the mid-point** between two features you want in the shot, and stand there.
3. **Keep things around you similar in size** — a tiny planet with wildly mismatched foreground/background objects looks messier, not better.
4. **Hold the camera low** so the horizon pops up above the middle of the frame rather than sitting flat across it.
5. **Leave breathing room** around the planet and keep it centred in frame.
6. Take five photos at each spot — small changes in height and position matter more than they look like they would.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. Apply the standard tiny planet reframe (FOV to max, tilt straight down) — see [Tiny Planet & Inverted Tiny Planet](tiny-planet-and-inverted.md).
2. Export at full resolution — never screenshot the preview, which throws away most of the detail.

## Settings
- Photo mode, 72 MP, ideally with HDR photo on for a bright sky (see [HDR / AEB Photo Recipe](hdr-aeb-photo-recipe.md)).

## Tips
- This is a composition guide, not a new reframe technique — pair it with the base tiny planet page and with [Walking Tiny Planet](walking-tiny-planet.md) for a moving version.
- Works especially well on the Maui trip's scenic viewpoints, where a wide landscape behind you gives plenty to work with.
