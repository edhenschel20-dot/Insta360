# Black / Red Sand Beach
Dark sand fools auto exposure into brightening the whole scene to grey mush. A small negative EV keeps the sand dark and the surf white.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Works on X5:** Yes

**[general advice]** — no creator tutorial specific to dark-sand beaches was found; based on general exposure practice for dark subjects.

## Shoot it
1. Set EV to −0.3 to −0.7 so the sand stays dark and the surf stays bright white against it.
2. White balance auto.
3. Sand is hard on lenses — wipe with a wet cloth and rinse, never dry-wipe. Carry the spare lens kit.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. No special reframe needed for a straight walking shot — the exposure fix is what matters here.
2. For a composed shot, try [Low-Angle Ground Skim](low-angle-ground-skim.md) over the wet sand — the sky reflects in it.
3. Or a [Tiny Planet](tiny-planet-composition.md), where the dark beach becomes a black planet with white surf round the rim (try inverted too, if it just looks like a hole the right way up).

## Tips
- The family walking away as silhouettes against the surf is a good use of the same exposure setting.
- Don't skip the lens-cleaning routine — sand is more damaging to a lens than salt water alone.
