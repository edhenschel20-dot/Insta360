# Spin One Photo Into Another
The "spin one photo into another" effect — there's no single one-tap template that does the whole thing, but two in-app pieces combine to get there: animate each still, then cut between them with a spin-style transition.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. Take a 360 photo of the scene/group you want to spin from (or into) — a photo works better than a clip for this, because Spin View and Animate are built for stills.
2. Keep the subject roughly centred and not too close to the lens — Spin View doesn't detect a subject, so it can crop a lone person out of frame; it works best on groups or landscapes (Ben Claremont rates Spin View C-tier for solo shots for this reason).
3. If you want the clip-to-clip version instead of photo-to-photo, shoot two ordinary clips as normal — nothing special needed at shoot time.

## Edit it (phone app)
**One-tap route (photos):**
1. Open the 360 photo in the app.
2. Tap **Animate** (added July 2026) and choose a camera movement — this gives the still its own spin/pan/zoom motion.
3. Or use Shot Lab › **Spin View** on the photo instead: pick from its 8 sub-templates (including "Circle of 3–6 people", "Sky" and "Tiny planet fade") and it animates the whole thing for you.
4. Drop the animated photo into Edit › Create a Video alongside your other clip/photo.
5. Tap the white box between the two items in the timeline and pick a transition from the library. Ben Claremont's picks for a spin-style cut are **Zoom Cut** and **Blur Cut** — the exact list of spin-named transitions isn't published and changes by version, so scroll the box in your app and try a few.

**Manual route (full control, works on clips too):**
1. On the end of clip/photo A, add a roll keyframe: 0° → 180° over the last 1–2 seconds, using a "Fade in / Quick out" curve so the spin accelerates into the cut.
2. On the start of clip/photo B, mirror it: 180° → 360°, same 1–2 second spacing, so the spin speed matches across the join.
3. Cut exactly on the blurriest part of the spin — that's what hides the seam.

## Settings
- Photos: 72 MP is plenty for Spin View / Animate.
- Clips: whatever resolution you already shot at — the roll keyframe trick doesn't need anything special.

## Tips
- Try the one-tap route first (Animate, then a transitions-library preset) — it's a 30-second job. Only go manual if the one-tap version doesn't sell the spin, or you're transitioning between two video clips rather than photos.
- See [Roll Planet / Spin View / Jump Planet](roll-planet-and-spin-view.md) for more on the one-tap Spin View template itself, and [Roll / Rotation Transition](roll-rotation-transition.md) for the clip-to-clip version of the manual method.
