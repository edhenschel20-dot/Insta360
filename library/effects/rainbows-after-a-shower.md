# Rainbows After a Shower
A rainbow across a coastal road or valley, with the family in the same frame — the 360 catches it without you having to find the right angle.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. A rainbow sits opposite the sun, so a 360 camera always has it somewhere in frame — you don't need to hunt for the angle.
2. EV −0.3, Vivid colour.
3. Keep the sun-side lens clean. The rainbow is on the other lens, so a smeared sun-side lens only costs you the family shot, not the rainbow.
4. Passing-shower rainbows come and go in minutes — if you're stopped anyway, a Timelapse at 11K, 2-second interval, catches it forming.
5. Shooting in drizzle: hold the camera low and shake it dry — the sky-facing lens spots up quickest.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. Reframe wide with you and the family in shot and the arc behind you.
2. Or a [Tiny Planet](tiny-planet-composition.md) with the arc as a ring.

## Settings
Full reference: [Timelapse and timeshift](../settings/timelapse-and-timeshift.md) for the 2-second-interval version.

## Tips
- There's no polariser option on the X5, so don't expect the rainbow to "pop" the way it does in a phone photo with one — reframe for the whole scene instead of chasing more saturation than the camera can give you.
- Worth doing as a quick stop on a road trip rather than trying to catch it from a moving car.
