# Ski & Snowboard Mount Map
A reference for which mount gives which look on the slopes, plus the two set-up rules that make a POV shot look good instead of shaky and pointless.

**Difficulty:** Medium (POV framing takes a couple of runs to get right) · **Works on X5:** Yes

## Shoot it
1. **Action Invisible Stick** (hand-held) → third-person view: hold it angled ahead of you so viewers see what's in front, not just your own back.
2. **Ski Pole Mount** → third-person view from the pole itself.
3. **Extended stick** → fake-drone overhead view (see [Fake Drone / Aerial Shot](fake-drone-shot.md)).
4. **Adhesive/helmet mount** → first-person POV. **Rule:** the helmet camera must be angled so it can actually see your skis/board and boots, or the POV reads as pointless sky-and-trees.
5. **Unicorn helmet mount** → a raised, multi-directional view above your head.
6. **Chest strap** → first-person POV, lower and steadier than a helmet mount.
7. Two Mic Air units let two skiers/riders each carry their own microphone into one X5's audio.

## Edit it (phone app)
1. For the helmet/chest POV: reframe with roughly zoom 120, dewarp 0.5, and turn Direction Lock on so the view stays forward through turns.
2. For the invisible-stick third-person view: one or two keyframes angled slightly ahead of you, adjusted with [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) if you're moving fast.

## Settings
- [Bright sun & snow settings](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md) for exposure on white slopes.

## Tips
- The single biggest difference between a good POV clip and a boring one is camera angle, not editing — get the helmet/chest mount tilted so it sees your skis before you set off.
- If you ever try it, the same mount logic (invisible stick = third-person, extended stick = fake drone, chest strap = first-person) carries straight over to golf and cycling.
