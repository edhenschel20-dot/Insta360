# Split and fixed views

The fastest fix when Auto Frame or AI Edit dwells on faces instead of the
action. No tracking, no keyframes — just cut the clip and pick a direction
for each piece.

## Steps
1. Watch (or skim) the clip and spot where the AI's suggested reframe
   spends too long turned toward you or the family instead of the action.
2. **Split the clip** at that point (menu names vary by app version — look
   for a scissors/split tool on the timeline).
3. On the piece that's mostly action (the ride, the fireworks, the view),
   apply a **Fixed view** pointed at the action. No keyframes needed.
4. On the short piece where the family reaction matters, apply its own
   **Fixed view** — this is the "quick glance at the family" piece. Keep it
   short; it's meant as a beat, not the main shot.
5. Export both pieces, or drop them back-to-back in your timeline if you're
   building a longer edit.

## Tips
- This works because Fixed view never tracks anything — it just always
  shows one direction, so it can't get pulled toward a face the way Auto
  Frame or Deep Track can.
- It's the right first thing to try before reaching for
  [Deep Track](deep-track.md) or [keyframes](keyframes-with-easing.md) —
  it's faster than either when a clip only needs "look at the action, then
  a quick look at the family."
- If the family reaction is worth more than a quick glance, use
  [Multi-view](multi-view.md) instead so you don't have to choose.
