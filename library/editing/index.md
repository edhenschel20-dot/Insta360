# Which reframing method should I use?

You let the camera roll and got a 360 sphere. Now you need to turn it into a
flat clip someone can watch. The app gives you a ladder of methods, roughly
fastest-to-slowest. **Start at the top and only drop down a rung if it
doesn't do what you need** — most clips don't need manual keyframes at all.

## The ladder (fastest first)

1. **Fixed view export** — pick a direction (Forward, Selfie, Custom), export.
   No keyframes at all. Use this whenever one direction covers the whole
   clip (a static event shot, a driving clip). Seconds of work.
2. **[Split and fixed views](split-and-fixed-views.md)** — if the AI dwells on
   faces for too long, split the clip into pieces and give each piece its own
   fixed view. This is the fastest fix when the default result isn't right,
   and doesn't need any tracking or keyframes.
3. **[Deep Track](deep-track.md)** — draw your own box around whoever or
   whatever you actually want followed. This is the only method where **you**
   pick the subject, not the AI. Use it for family events, a rider on a
   trail, anything moving that you want the camera to follow.
4. **[Multi-view](multi-view.md)** — show two to four angles at once (e.g.
   front view plus a reaction shot) instead of choosing one. Good when you
   don't want to pick.
5. **[Keyframes with easing](keyframes-with-easing.md)** — two keyframes plus
   Ease In/Out for a deliberate camera move (a reveal, a crane shot). Use
   this when you want a specific, cinematic move, not just "follow this
   thing."
6. **[Quick Edit: steer by phone](quick-edit-steer-by-phone.md)** — play the
   clip and steer live by moving your phone or swiping. One pass, no
   keyframes, done in real time. Good for walk-throughs and look-arounds
   where you just want to "look around" naturally.
7. **[Tiny planet](tiny-planet.md)** — a one-tap look for group shots and
   static scenes. Not a general reframe method, but worth knowing as a
   one-tap option.
8. **[Auto Frame and AI Edit](auto-frame-and-ai-edit.md)** — let the AI do the
   whole thing. Fast, but it **prefers people over the action** and will
   often dwell on faces when you wanted the fireworks, the ride, or the
   view. Use it as a starting point to triage a big pile of clips, not as
   the final answer for anything you actually care about.

## Before any of this: finding the good bits
If you've got a long continuous roll, don't scrub through the whole thing
by hand. See [Finding moments in long clips](finding-moments-in-long-clips.md)
for voice marks, markers, and AI Highlights.

!!! tip "The habit that saves the most time"
    If the AI keeps swinging round to show you holding the camera, that's
    expected — you're always in the 360 capture. Don't try to stay out of
    shot. Just don't let the edit dwell there: pick Deep Track or split-and-
    fixed-views instead of Auto Frame/AI Edit for anything with people in it.
