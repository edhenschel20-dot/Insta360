# Finding moments in long clips

You let it roll for an hour or more. Here's how to find the good bits
without scrubbing through the whole thing by hand.

## Steps
1. **While shooting: say "Mark that"** out loud whenever something notable
   happens (voice control works even with the screen off, within about
   1.2 m of the camera). It costs nothing in the moment.
2. There are other ways to mark too: tapping a flag on the camera screen,
   or Twist-to-Shoot on the stick, if voice isn't convenient.
3. **Marks are written into the clip file itself** and show up on the
   timeline in Insta360 Studio (desktop) — open the clip there first to see
   them if you're not sure where the good moments are.
4. **Turn on AI Highlights Assistant** and leave it on by default — it
   automatically flags exciting moments during recording and delivers a
   ready-made highlight reel in the app once you reconnect to the phone.
   Note: it's incompatible with Endurance Mode, Stereo Recording, and 360
   Audio, so using Endurance Mode to stretch battery life means losing this
   automatic help on that footage.
5. **Before running a long roll through Auto Edit**, check its total
   length — see [Auto Frame and AI Edit](auto-frame-and-ai-edit.md) for the
   footage-length limits. A 90+ minute continuous take exceeds even the
   high-end-phone ceiling and needs a rough pre-trim first (even just
   "keep the middle 45 minutes" is enough).

## Tips
- The mark habit is the single cheapest thing you can do at shoot time —
  it costs nothing and makes editing noticeably faster later.
- Don't fight long clips by scrubbing through them manually. Marks, AI
  Highlights, and a rough pre-trim before Auto Edit cover this without
  needing to shoot shorter in the first place.
- **"The Long Take":** one continuous roll can be reconfigured entirely in
  post — different segments given different direction, FOV and speed —
  rather than being treated as one fixed shot. A good mental model for why
  letting it roll is worth the extra minutes of raw footage.
- Jordan Hetrick's "why I still film everything": the same argument as this
  project's own "things happen behind you" reasoning — long, unbroken
  clips catch moments you'd otherwise miss by stopping and starting.
