# People Photos with a Phone

For when someone hands you their phone. You've got about ten seconds and one chance — these are the moves that make a phone photo stand out with no settings changes, just angle, lens and timing.

## The low angle — and why it can backfire
Crouch or put the phone near the ground, tilt up, subject filling the frame with sky or ceiling behind them. It makes people look taller and heroic, and the background turns into clean sky instead of clutter.

**But** for a tight head-and-shoulders shot, a low angle shoots up the nostrils and emphasises the chin. Keep the low angle for full-body shots; for head shots go eye level or a touch above, and say "chin forward and down," not "chin up."

For the full-body version, rest the bottom edge of the phone right on the ground so it looks deliberate, not accidental — use the volume button as the shutter so you don't tilt it.

## 0.5x ultrawide, close in
Switch to 0.5x and step in until the subject is about 1 m away, positioned in the middle third of the frame. Ultrawide exaggerates whatever's close and shrinks the background — the strongest combination for "look at this place" shots. Avoid putting faces at the frame edge (they stretch), and don't go closer than about 1 m for a head shot.

## 0.5x from above, for groups
Hold the phone up high at 0.5x, tilt slightly down. Everyone's face is visible, nobody's hidden behind a shoulder, and it's the safest group angle when there's no time to arrange people. Don't tilt too much or foreheads dominate and feet disappear.

## Tiny person, huge place
At 0.5x, put the person small in the frame with the landscape or architecture as the real subject — reads like a travel photo. Do this as the second or third shot, not the only one, if the person specifically wants themselves in the picture.

## Shoot through something
Put a leaf, railing, doorway, arch, shoulder or glass between you and the subject, very close to the lens, and tap the subject to focus. The soft blurred foreground adds depth with no Portrait Mode fakery. Don't let it cover the face.

## Backlight, tap the face
Put the low sun just behind the subject's head or shoulder, tap the face on screen, and if it's dark, drag the exposure slider up a touch. Rim light on hair and shoulders separates them from the background — probably the single biggest jump in "does this look professional." Avoid the sun directly behind the torso (it washes everything out) and harsh midday overhead sun (turn them into open shade instead).

## Shade with a bright background
In harsh sun, stand the subject just inside shade facing out toward the light, with the sunny scene behind them. Same look as backlight, works at any hour.

## Portrait Mode: when it helps and when it lies
Use it when the subject is still, well lit, and clearly in front of a background some distance away — check the edges before handing the phone back. Avoid it for frizzy hair, glasses, hats, hands holding things, busy backgrounds, movement or low light; it cuts hair off and draws halos. If in doubt, take one normal shot and one Portrait Mode shot.

## Get real blur instead
Step in close (1x, or the tele lens if there is one) and put real distance between the person and the background — this is how actual lenses blur backgrounds, no fakery needed.

## Posing prompts instead of "smile"
- "Walk toward me, don't look at me… now look at me."
- "Look at each other and tell them something embarrassing about yourself."
- "Look over your shoulder, then look at me on three."
- Solo: "Fix your hair / look down at your feet / now look up."

One prompt per shot — movement kills the stiff pose.

## Micro-posing that always helps
Weight on the back foot, front knee soft. Hands doing something (pocket, bag strap, coffee), not hanging. Chin slightly forward and down — the one thing that rescues a low angle. Groups: shoulders angled in, varying head heights.

## Take three variations, every time
1. The shot they asked for, straight, eye level.
2. A low-angle or 0.5x version.
3. A tighter shot, or a candid taken right after they think you're done.

Hand the phone back saying "I took a few, pick your favourite" — the second choice is usually the one they actually post, and it costs ten seconds.

## Burst and Live Photo for candids
Tap to lock focus, then use burst for jumps, laughs and kids. Live Photo is the fallback for blinks. Don't leave dozens of burst frames on someone's phone — say "keep the best, delete the rest."

## Small things that matter
- **Clean the lens** on your shirt before the first frame — a greasy lens is the #1 cause of hazy photos.
- **Tap and hold to lock focus/exposure**, then recompose off-centre.
- **Keep the horizon straight** unless you're deliberately tilting for effect.
- **Reflections** — puddle, shop window, wet table — get the lens right down to the surface for an instant "how did you do that" shot.
- **Silhouette** as a bonus frame at sunset: tap the sky instead of the face.
