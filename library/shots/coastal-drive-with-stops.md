# Coastal drive with stops

A scenic coastal road with frequent pull-offs — waterfalls, viewpoints, the
odd rainbow after a shower. The trick is treating the driving and the stops
as two different shots, not one continuous clip. **Plant-and-go at every
stop, TimeShift or normal video for the road between them.**

## See it
No single verified video covers this whole plan; it's built from a
travel-tips video and separate waterfall/rainbow examples (see Sources).
The travel tips: [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kzm9k4SJN7A&t=125s).

## Before you leave (5 minutes)
- [ ] **Charge the battery**, and bring a spare or a power bank — a long
  day of stops adds up.
- [ ] **Check card space** for a full day of driving plus several stops.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses** — waterfall spray is the main hazard today.
- [ ] Keep the stick with its mini tripod screwed on permanently, living
  somewhere easy to grab — the door pocket works well — so every stop is a
  ten-second set-up, not a repack. See
  [Plant-and-Go Stick Tripod](../effects/plant-and-go-stick-tripod.md).

## Settings (set once for the road, change once per stop)
| | |
|---|---|
| Driving | **TimeShift**, mounted on the dash or windscreen |
| At a stop | Switch to standard **Video, 5.7K30**, EV **−0.3 to −0.7** if there's spray or a rainbow to protect |
| White balance | Auto |

See [Timelapse & TimeShift settings](../settings/timelapse-and-timeshift.md)
for the driving stretches and
[Fast motion & driving settings](../settings/fast-motion-and-driving.md)
for the road in general.

## Where to stand and how to hold it
- **Driving:** mount on the dash or windscreen, lens axis along the
  direction of travel — one lens forward through the windscreen, one lens
  back into the cabin.
- **At a waterfall or viewpoint:** the stick with its mini tripod already
  attached — pop the legs, stand it, and you're filming again in ten
  seconds. Hold it out toward the spray if you want to get close to a
  waterfall (see [Waterfall Plunge-Pool](../effects/waterfall-plunge-pool.md));
  rinse and shake the lenses dry afterwards rather than wiping them with a
  finger.
- **Rainbows:** keep the sun-side lens especially clean — the rainbow sits
  opposite the sun, so a smeared sun-side lens costs you your family's
  faces, not the rainbow. See [Rainbows After a Shower](../effects/rainbows-after-a-shower.md).
- **Walking with kids at a stop:** hold the camera at their eye height —
  see [Kid-Height Invisible Cinematographer](../effects/kid-height-invisible-cinematographer.md).

## While you're enjoying it
- **Film more than you think.** Ten to twenty minute clips at each stop,
  not quick clips — this isn't a "clip-style" camera.
- Keep the camera at roughly the kids' eye height when walking with them
  at a stop, rather than up over your head — the scene shows up in the
  frame instead of just the ground.
- Say **"Mark that"** at anything worth finding again.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- A tiny planet or wide shot with the family in frame and a rainbow behind
  them, if a passing shower gives you one.
- If you're stopped and a shower is passing, Timelapse at a 2-second
  interval catches a rainbow forming.

## Edit it fast
1. **TimeShift needs no reframing at all** — trim only.
2. Stops get the normal ladder: start with
   [Split & Fixed Views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md), or
   [Keyframes with Easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md) for a slow
   reveal at a viewpoint.
3. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) on a child walking ahead at a
   stop, rather than hand keyframes.

## Tips
- Don't try to make one continuous clip cover both driving and stopping —
  switch modes deliberately at each stop.
- Folders per day, backed up each evening, keep a long multi-stop day from
  turning into an unsorted mess later.
