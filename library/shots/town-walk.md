# Walking through a town

A slow wander through a small town: Pāʻia's surf shops, Makawao's
cowboy-town main street, Wailuku's Market Street, or Front Street in Lahaina.
Shops, murals, shave ice, and the kids peering into windows. The plan: **one
long walking clip at chest height, plus one or two 60-second set pieces.**

## Before you leave (2 minutes)
- [ ] Wipe both lenses.
- [ ] Battery at least half full. Town walks are 1–2 hours of stop-start.
- [ ] Stick with mini tripod. Bring the foldable handle too if you'll go
  into shops. See [Foldable Utility Handle](../effects/foldable-utility-handle.md).

## Settings (set once)
| | |
|---|---|
| Daytime | **Video 5.7K30**, EV 0, white balance Auto |
| Shady street / inside shops | Same. The X5 copes, so don't change it |
| After dark | **PureVideo**, 5.7K30, EV −0.3 to −0.7. See [Night & low light](../settings/night-and-low-light.md) |

Full reference: [Travel & City](travel-and-city.md).

## Where to hold it and how
- **Walking:** stick at about chest height, held a little out in front and
  to the side, **lenses pointing along the street** (shops on one side,
  road on the other). The camera sees where you're going and the family
  walking with you.
- **Short stick for crowded pavements**, at about a third of its length.
  Fully extended on a busy sidewalk is awkward for other people.
- **Into a shop:** collapse the stick to a handle, or just stop recording.
  Small shops often don't want filming, so ask if in doubt.

## While you're enjoying it
- Press record and let it roll from one end of the street to the other.
- Say **"Mark that"** at a mural, a funny sign, or the kids' faces at the shave
  ice counter.
- Stop walking now and then. A few seconds standing still gives the edit
  calm moments between the walking.

## Set pieces (pick one per town, 60 seconds each)
1. **Look-up shot under a tree:** walk slowly under a big banyan or monkeypod
   tree with the camera held low and pointed up. In the edit, the branches
   close in overhead. See [Look-Up Shot](../effects/look-up-shot.md).
2. **Street lapse:** stand the stick on its tripod on the pavement edge (not
   in anyone's way) and record **Timelapse** for 2 minutes while people
   stream past. See [Street Lapse & Flash Dash](../effects/street-lapse-and-flash-dash.md).
3. **Mural pivot:** walk a slow half-circle round a mural or landmark with
   it pinned in the frame, then speed it up. See
   [Landmark Pivot Hyperlapse](../effects/landmark-pivot-hyperlapse.md).
4. **Same pose, many towns:** do the same pose (for example, a spin with the
   stick held up) in front of a sign in each town you visit, then cut them
   together at the end of the trip. See [Same Pose, Many Places](../effects/same-pose-many-places.md).

## Town-by-town notes
- **Pāʻia:** a colourful, busy main street. Park once and walk. It's the
  natural first stop on the Road to Hana morning.
- **Makawao:** wooden storefronts and a quieter street. Good for the
  walking clip.
- **Wailuku:** Market Street shops and murals. Good for the mural pivot.
- **Lahaina:** Front Street reopened to traffic on 1 August 2026, but much of
  the town is still being rebuilt after the 2023 fire, and Banyan Court and
  the old courthouse were still fenced off in August 2026. **Point the camera
  at the open businesses and the people you're supporting, not at empty or
  damaged lots.** Locals have asked visitors not to sightsee the damage. Pick
  the fixed view in the edit so you only keep what you mean to.

## Edit it fast
1. **Fixed forward view** for the walking. It's the quickest, and it looks
   like a gimbal. See [Split & Fixed Views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md).
2. Split at each "mark that" and turn that short piece towards the thing
   (the mural, the kids). See [Keyframes with Easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md)
   if you want a smooth turn.
3. Use **AI Edit** only if you'll trim its people-heavy picks. In towns, the
   people often are the subject, so it does better here than at a parade.

## Tips
- Midday sun on a street gives harsh shadows. Late afternoon is kinder, and
  the shops are still open.
- Ask before filming people up close, such as shop owners, buskers or the
  shave ice server. They're in the 360 either way, but choose your framing
  in the edit with that in mind.
