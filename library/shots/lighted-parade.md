# Lighted parade at dusk

A parade with lit-up floats, starting while there's still some light in the
sky and getting darker as it goes. The whole plan: **set it up once, let it
roll, enjoy the parade.**

## Before you leave (5 minutes)
- [ ] **Charge the battery**, especially after a day of shooting. PureVideo drains it faster. Bring the spare.
- [ ] **Check card space.** About 1.5 hours of parade at 5.7K is roughly 120 GB, worst case.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses.** Lights plus a smudge give big glowing halos.
- [ ] Bring the mini tripod or use the stick's stand, if you have one.

## Settings (set once, before it starts)
| | |
|---|---|
| Mode | **PureVideo** |
| Resolution | **5.7K 30fps** (or 4K 30fps) |
| EV | **−0.7** to start |
| White balance | Auto |
| Colour | Standard |

Why −0.7: the float lights are small bright spots on a dark scene, and
negative EV stops them blowing out into white blobs. Creators disagree on
the exact number (anything from −0.3 to −3), so treat −0.7 as a starting
point. See [Night & low light](../settings/night-and-low-light.md).

## Where to stand and how to hold it
- **Curbside, with the lenses facing the street, not side-on.** That keeps
  the floats off the stitch seam.
- **Best option: the stick planted on a mini tripod at the curb**, raised to
  about head height. The camera stays perfectly still, which matters most at
  night: a still camera plus moving floats gives sharp floats and nice light
  streaks, while a moving camera in the dark gives blur.
- **Handheld option:** hold the stick still at chest to head height. Brace
  your elbow against your body. Don't wave it around.
- **Be considerate with the stick.** Holding it high over your head blocks
  the view of people behind you. Head height is fine, and a planted stick at
  the curb is best.

## While you're enjoying it
- **Press record when the first float appears, and let it roll.**
- Say **"Mark that"** at the best floats, or when your son lights up at
  something. It makes them easy to find later.
- That's it. Watch the parade.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- **Kid's-eye view:** for one float, lower the camera to your son's height.
  Floats look huge from down there. Covered in [Low-Angle Ground Skim](../effects/low-angle-ground-skim.md).
- **Light-streak timelapse:** if you have the tripod, put the camera in
  **Timelapse** mode (not PureVideo) and leave it running still for one
  stretch of the parade. The float lights turn into streaks. See
  [Night Interval Timelapse](../effects/night-interval-timelapse-72mp.md) and
  [City Light-Trail Drive-Lapse](../effects/city-light-trail-drive-lapse.md)
  for the idea.
- **Tiny planet photo** of the street before it starts, while there's still
  light in the sky: stick planted, photo mode, self-timer, hold still.
- If there are **fireworks** at the end, see [Fireworks Two-Parter](../effects/fireworks-two-parter.md).
- **Between floats:** see [Parade Periscope & Lamp-Post Clamp](../effects/parade-periscope-lamp-post-clamp.md) —
  raise the stick over the crowd for a "drone"-style pass, or clamp the
  camera to a lamp-post or barrier and let it roll hands-free rather than
  roaming with it.

## Edit it fast (tomorrow)
1. **Skip AI Edit and Auto Frame.** They'll swing round to faces instead
   of the floats.
2. **Fastest:** one fixed view pointed down the street, then export. See
   [Split & Fixed Views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md).
3. **Want a quick glance at your son's reaction?** Split the clip where he
   reacts, point that short piece at him, then back to the street. Or use
   [Multi-View](../editing/multi-view.md) for parade and reaction side by side.
4. **Follow one favourite float** with [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md),
   drawing the box on the float yourself.
5. **Compare** a stretch shot at −0.7 against any at 0 if you tried both, to
   learn what EV does in practice.

## Tips
- Dusk looks best. The first 20 minutes, while the sky is still deep blue,
  usually beat full dark.
- Don't use Bullet Time or slow-motion tonight. There's not enough light.
- Night is cooler, so overheating is unlikely even on a long roll.
