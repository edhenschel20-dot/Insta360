# Hiking & Views

The walk up, and the payoff at the top. Long clips are a feature here — things happen off to the side or behind you on a trail that you'd only notice later.

## Setup (once)
- Handheld or short stick at chest height while walking; switch to the extended invisible stick once you're at the viewpoint.
- Keep a steady pace and constant height on the approach — that's what makes the walking part fast to edit later.

## While you're enjoying it
- Walk and enjoy the view. Say "mark" at the summit, a good vista, or wildlife.

## Edit it fast
- Let the whole hike roll as one long clip; use [Finding Moments in Long Clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md) to pull out the approach and the payoff rather than deciding in advance where one ends and the other begins.
- At the viewpoint, a slow manual pan (two keyframes) or a [Tiny Planet](../editing/tiny-planet.md) still both work well — see [Keyframes with Easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md) for the pan.

## Effects that work here
- [360 Look-Around (Slow Pan)](../effects/look-around-pan.md) — the classic summit reveal.
- [Tiny Planet & Inverted](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) — a shareable still from the top.
- [Hyperlapse / TimeShift](../effects/hyperlapse-and-timeshift.md) — long, steady stretches of trail.

## When you want a hero shot
- [Rise Shot (Crane)](../effects/rise-shot-crane.md) at the summit for a reveal that lifts up and over the view.
