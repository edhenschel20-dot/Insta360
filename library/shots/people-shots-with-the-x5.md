# People Shots with the X5

Two ideas underpin most of these: **camera height is the shot** (low down, everyone's a giant; high up, everyone's a figure in a landscape — eye level is the least interesting height for 360), and **the stick is invisible, so the stick is the crane, the dolly, the tripod and the drone.**

## The giant shot
Stick at 20–40 cm off the ground (or the X5 on its mini tripod, timer 3–5 s), lens pointed at the person 1–2 m away. Ask them to step in and look down at it, or stride over it. Reframe wide, tilt up to the face, keep a bit of ground in shot. Best full-body — same chin-forward-and-down tip applies for tight faces as on a phone.

## Camera on the ground, everyone leans in
X5 flat on the ground or a table on its mini tripod, timer 5 s, group leans in around it, heads meeting above the lens. Reframe straight up for a classic "circle of faces," or use [Tiny Planet & Inverted](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) for a ring of people standing on a ball. One capture, two completely different portraits — and you're in it too.

## Tiny planet group portrait
Stick planted vertical, camera 1–1.5 m up, group in a loose circle 2–3 m out, all facing the camera. Open sky above, something patterned on the ground (tiles, sand, a plaza) — the ground becomes the planet. See [Tiny Planet & Inverted](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) for the edit.

## Jump Planet and other Shot Lab jump templates
Extended invisible stick, everyone jumps on cue, the app uses the jump as the trigger for a tiny-planet transition. See [Roll Planet / Spin View / Jump Planet](../effects/roll-planet-and-spin-view.md). Worth a few minutes scrolling Shot Lab before a trip so you know what's currently there.

## The overhead invisible-stick "drone" portrait
Stick fully extended (1.2 m standard, 3 m with the Extended Edition) straight up and slightly forward, everyone below. Reframe straight down, wide FOV, for a top-down cluster shot, or angle 45° down for a "drone at height" look. See [Fake Drone Shot](../effects/fake-drone-shot.md) and [Third-Person Follow / Top-Down View](../effects/third-person-follow-view.md).

## The rise-and-reveal (stick as crane)
Start with the camera at knee height in front of the person, smoothly raise the stick overhead over about 3 seconds while stepping back half a pace. Keyframe from a face shot at the start to a top-down view at the top. See [Rise Shot (Crane)](../effects/rise-shot-crane.md). The reverse — start high, dive down to the face — works as an intro.

## The walk-past / orbit (stick as dolly)
Person stands still; walk a slow half-circle around them at 2–3 m, stick at waist height. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) keeps them centred (they should be the only clear subject). See [360 Orbit (Hand-Swung)](../effects/hand-swung-360-orbit.md).

## Bullet time without throwing
Options: the classic overhead swing on the Bullet Time handle (about 1 rotation per second, one lens up, one down); holding the stick horizontal and spinning on the spot yourself; or swinging the camera around a friend who stands still so they're the orbited subject. Good light only. See [Bullet Time](../effects/bullet-time.md).

## Between-lenses "two worlds" split
Plant the stick between two people or things — one facing each lens. Use [Split Screen / MultiView](../effects/split-screen-multiview.md) — no tracking needed.

## Doorway / tunnel reveal
Walk through a doorway, gate, or trees with the stick overhead. Keyframe looking up at the frame as you pass, then tilt down to the person and the view opening beyond. See [Look-Up Shot](../effects/look-up-shot.md).

## Through-the-legs / under the table
X5 on the ground between or beside people's feet (dinner table, picnic), record stationary. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) whoever's talking, or reframe up through the table edge or knees — a viewpoint nobody normally sees, and the least intrusive way to film people naturally.

## Handshake / hug from inside the moment
Short stick (30 cm), camera held between two people as they hug, high-five, or clink glasses. Wide FOV, centred on the hands/faces — the camera is inside the action rather than watching it.

## Enter-the-planet opening
Any tiny-planet capture with the group: keyframe from zoomed-out tiny planet to normal view on the faces. See [Tiny Planet Flip / Planet Landing](../effects/tiny-planet-flip-transition.md). Makes a good 4-second title card for a longer edit.

## Ski-pole / bike / backpack third-person rig
Stick clamped to a ski pole, backpack strap, or bike, 1 m+ behind and above. See [Third-Person Follow / Top-Down View](../effects/third-person-follow-view.md) and [Front-Mount "Who Is Filming Me"](../effects/front-mount-pov.md).

## Dolly zoom on a friend
Walk toward the person at a steady speed, camera at their chest height. See [Dolly Zoom / Horizon Pull](../effects/dolly-zoom.md) — 360 makes it trivial since FOV is a slider, not a lens swap.

## Clone Trail / "same person three times"
Static camera, the friend moves through several positions, the app composites the copies. See [Clone Trail](../effects/clone-trail.md) — a static camera means you can step out of shot.

## Sky Swap for the flat grey day
Fixes a boring sky on any tiny-planet or overhead group shot. See [Sky Swap](../effects/sky-swap.md). Use sparingly — an obviously swapped sky looks like what it is.

## Time Freeze portrait
One 72 MP photo, ideally mid-gesture, animated into a flying-camera clip. See [Time Freeze (Animated Photo)](../effects/time-freeze-animated-photo.md) — pairs well with the enter-the-planet opening above as an intro.

## The self-portrait "how did you get that?" shot
Stick vertical straight above your head, fully extended, looking up, on patterned ground. Tiny Planet makes you appear standing on the top of your own planet — or reframe straight down at max FOV for a "floating drone selfie." No visible stick, no drone, no helper.
