# Aerial & Helicopter

Not covered directly in this project's research — the notes below are general advice for shooting 360 video from a small aircraft, marked **[general advice]** throughout. Follow the operator's safety rules over anything written here.

## Setup (once) [general advice]
- Handheld with a wrist or neck strap is safer than a stick in a moving aircraft — you don't want anything that could be pulled out of an open door or window.
- Ask the operator where you're allowed to hold the camera and whether a window needs to stay closed; some doors-off flights have their own camera rules.
- If shooting through a window, expect reflections — angle the lens as close to the glass as the operator allows, without touching it, to cut down on glare.

## While you're enjoying it [general advice]
- Hold the camera steady and enjoy the flight. Say "mark" at anything you want to find easily afterwards (a landmark, a good bank/turn).

## Edit it fast [general advice]
- A fixed downward or forward view (see [Split & Fixed Views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md)) is usually enough — there's rarely a single tracked subject in an aerial shot.
- [Tiny Planet & Inverted](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) on a hover or level stretch makes a good still.

## Effects that work here
- [Third-Person Follow / Top-Down View](../effects/third-person-follow-view.md) — the overhead view is effectively built in from altitude.
- [Tiny Planet & Inverted](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md) — coastline or city views make dramatic planets.

## Settings
[Bright sun & snow settings](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md) generally applies at altitude in daylight; [Fast motion & driving settings](../settings/fast-motion-and-driving.md) if the aircraft is moving fast (e.g. a fast bank or descent).
