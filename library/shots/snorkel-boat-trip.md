# Snorkel boat trip

The camera keeps rolling from the boat deck, through the entry into the
water, and onto whatever's down there — a turtle, a fish, or nothing much,
which is fine. **Start it before you get in, don't stop for the entry.**

## Before you leave (5 minutes)
- [ ] **Save a 5.7K60 preset to the Q/power button** ahead of time — once
  the dive case is sealed you can't change most settings, so set this
  before you leave, not on the boat.
- [ ] **Charge the battery** — you can't swap it once the case is sealed.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses** and check the dive case seal.
- [ ] Bring a floaty handle for kids who'll hold the camera themselves.

## Settings (set once, before you board)
| | |
|---|---|
| Mode | Standard Video, in the Invisible Dive Case |
| Resolution | **5.7K 60fps** |
| Exposure | Auto |
| Colour | AquaVision applied afterwards, in the edit |

## Where to stand and how to hold it
- **Press record before you sit on the boat's edge or tube, and hold the
  camera to your chest for the entry.** Extend the stick once you're in
  the water.
- No dive case needed for surface snorkelling in calm water; use it if
  you'll be diving down or swimming through anything rougher.
- Kids: the same, from the swim step, holding a floaty handle rather than
  a stick.

## While you're enjoying it
- **Just let it run.** This is one of the few situations where you don't
  need to aim at anything — swim with it, dunk it, let it roll.
- Say **"Mark that"** (or use a physical marker if your voice won't
  register underwater) when something worth finding later happens.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- If something swims past behind you that nobody noticed, that's the point
  of letting long clips roll — check for it with
  [Finding Moments in Long Clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md)
  rather than trying to catch it live.

## Edit it fast
1. Mostly triage: skim for the good stretches with
   [Finding Moments in Long Clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md).
2. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) only on anything specific worth
   following — a diver, a turtle, a fellow swimmer.
3. Otherwise a fixed forward view is enough; no need to reframe a whole
   snorkel session.

## Tips
- Letting it roll for the whole entry, not just the water, is what catches
  the boat chatter and the first fish in one clip instead of two.
- If a boat crew member calls something out (a turtle, a shark), that's a
  good moment to say "mark" rather than to move the camera toward it.
