# Whale watch

A breach and your family's reaction, in one clip, from a moving boat —
without chasing the whale around with the camera. **Plant it, let it roll,
don't turn it.**

## Before you leave (5 minutes)
- [ ] **Use the black camera body if you have one**, or keep the dive
  housing off while on deck. A white X5 body reflects inside a dive
  housing in this footage.
- [ ] **Charge the battery** — plan for the whole trip out and back, not
  just the sighting window.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses** — sea spray is constant on deck.
- [ ] Bring a leash or wrist strap; don't rely on grip alone over water.

## Settings (set once, before you leave the dock)
| | |
|---|---|
| Mode | Standard Video |
| Resolution | **5.7K 60fps** (higher frame rate in case you want to slow a breach) |
| EV | Auto, adjust for glare off the water |
| White balance | Auto |

See [Bright sun & snow](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md) for glare-heavy
days.

## Where to stand and how to hold it
- **Stand at the back of the boat** — the most stable spot — and lean your
  hips against the rail rather than fighting the boat's movement with your
  arms.
- Stick planted in the mini tripod on the rail, or held steady at chest
  height, lens toward the water on the side the captain calls.
- **Don't move the camera to chase a splash or a spout.** Anticipate where
  the whale will surface and let the 360 capture it — moving the camera
  around loses more than it gains, and a fixed frame with a wide field of
  view already has both the whale and your family's faces in it.

## While you're enjoying it
- **Press record once you're underway, and let it roll** — whales surface
  behind you as often as in front.
- Say **"Mark that"** the moment something breaches or surfaces.
- Watch it with your family; don't watch it through the screen.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- If a dolphin or shark shows up alongside, the same fixed, rolling clip
  already has it — nothing extra to do.

## Edit it fast
1. **Skip AI Edit** for the same reason as everywhere else on this trip: it
   prefers people over the action.
2. [Multi-View](../editing/multi-view.md) for the whale and your family's
   faces at once — this is the shot this technique is made for.
3. [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) on the whale itself, but only
   after it's surfaced — trying to track open water beforehand doesn't
   give it anything to lock onto.

## Tips
- A calm day helps more than any setting — pick one if you get a choice of
  sailing times.
- 120fps sounds tempting for a breach, but a whale surfacing is often 30–60
  seconds of very little happening; 60fps is a better balance of slow-mo
  headroom and file size.
