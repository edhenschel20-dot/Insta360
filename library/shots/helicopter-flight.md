# Helicopter flight (doors on)

Through-window aerial views without reflections of your own T-shirt, and
the whole cabin — family reactions included — from one camera. **Mount it
once, let it roll for the flight.**

## See it
No verified 360 tutorial exists for a doors-on tour flight specifically;
the technique below is built from general aerial-photography advice plus
X5 cabin-mounting videos (see Sources). Nate Fishler's doors-off X2 tour is
a look-reference only, not a tutorial:
[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9tl7U6lKuhg).

## Before you leave (the day before)
- [ ] **Ask the operator about camera rules before the flight** — some
  tour operators restrict loose items, mounts, or where a camera can be
  held or placed. Confirm doors-on/doors-off and any suction-mount policy.
- [ ] **Charge the battery and bring a spare** — no swapping mid-flight.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses.**
- [ ] Pack a wrist strap or tether for anything handheld.

## Settings (set once, before boarding)
| | |
|---|---|
| Mode | Standard Video |
| Resolution | **5.7K 30fps**, or 8K if the operator allows it and card space is available |
| EV | Auto to start; adjust for bright sky/glare once airborne |
| White balance | Auto |

See [Bright sun & snow](../settings/bright-sun-and-snow.md).

## Where to stand and how to hold it
- **Get the near lens as close to the window glass as you're allowed** —
  reflections come from the cabin, not the glass, so closing that gap is
  the substitute for a lens hood (which isn't possible on a 360 camera).
  The far lens gets the cabin, which is fine — that's the shot too.
- **Wear dark clothing** near the window; it reduces what reflects.
- **Tether everything.** Never rely on a suction mount outside the
  aircraft — pressure changes rule that out. Inside, a suction mount on
  the window or canopy works if the operator allows it; otherwise hold the
  stick or the camera itself against the glass.
- If you're allowed to hold it loose, keep a wrist strap on at all times.

## While you're enjoying it
- **Press record before lift-off, and let it roll for the whole flight** —
  don't stop and start for each view.
- Say **"Mark that"** at anything worth finding again — a landmark, a
  reaction.
- Watch the view. The 360 already has the cabin behind you.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- If your headset has an ear-cup mic input, clean intercom audio is
  possible that way — separate to the camera's own audio.

## Edit it fast
1. **Skip AI Edit and Auto Frame** for the same reason as everywhere else:
   they tend to swing back to faces in the cabin.
2. **Fastest:** a fixed view out of the window. See
   [Split & Fixed Views](../editing/split-and-fixed-views.md).
3. Want the cabin too? [Multi-View](../editing/multi-view.md) for the view
   and the family's faces side by side.
4. A slow zoom-out or pan on the window view reads well for scenic aerial
   footage — see [Keyframes with Easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md).

## Tips
- A fixed view or a single long pan usually works best for scenic aerial
  footage — there's rarely a reason to do more.
- The operator's rules come first, every time; don't improvise a mount
  they haven't approved.
