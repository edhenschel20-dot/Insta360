# Being there: 360 photos and headset clips

Most of what you share will be **flat videos made from your 360 footage**.
That's what people actually watch. But a few moments are worth keeping
**as 360**, so you (or anyone willing) can look all the way round and feel
like you're back there.

## The short version
1. **Keep shooting 360 video all the time.** It's what lets you choose the
   view later. Share flat edits.
2. **Keep every original file.** They *are* the 360. The app keeps getting
   better, so you can re-edit them in a year. Back them up to Immich (see
   [Handling footage while travelling](../gear/data-while-travelling.md)).
3. **One 360 photo at each special place.** About 30 seconds.
4. **Three to five "headset clips"** on the whole trip: camera planted and
   still for a minute at the very best moments.

## 1. The 360 "being there" photo
Why a photo and not just video: it's **far sharper** (72MP against a video
frame), anyone can look round it without a headset, and nobody gets
queasy looking at a still picture.

**How (about 30 seconds, when everyone has stopped anyway):**
1. Stop the video. Switch to **Photo** mode (swipe on the screen to
   change mode, or make a **PHOTO** preset on the Q button, see
   [Quick set-ups](../settings/quick-setups.md)).
2. Resolution **72MP**. If the camera's on the **2-in-1 as a tripod**, turn
   on **HDR Photo** for bright skies; it needs the camera still. Holding the
   stick? Plain Photo is fine.
3. Put the camera **at about head height, or just above**, on the stick.
   Too high looks like a drone; too low looks like a dog's view.
4. Everyone looks natural. **Hold still**, press the shutter.
5. **Switch back to Video** and start rolling again. Don't forget this
   step.

**Forgot to take one?** The app can grab a still from a 360 video
(**Snapshot** in the editor). It's lower resolution than a real photo, but
it's better than nothing.

## 2. Headset clips (a few, very carefully)
People get motion sick in a headset when **the camera moves**: walking,
driving, a boat bobbing. A camera that stays still is comfortable and feels
like standing there.

**How:**
- Plant the **2-in-1 as a tripod**, legs out, at the highest setting you
  can. Don't hold it.
- **Video, 8K30** for these clips only (sharper in a headset). Your
  everyday 5.7K30 is fine if the card or battery is low. They aren't for
  Shot Lab templates, so 8K doesn't matter.
- Keep people and things **at least an arm's length** from the camera.
  Anything closer looks huge and can stitch badly.
- Let it roll **30–60 seconds**, then pick it up. Nobody touches it while
  it's running.

**Best moments for this:** the summit sunrise above the clouds, a Road to
Hana waterfall, a sunset on the beach, and the reef in the dive case
(stay as still as you can, let the fish come to you).

## Showing them off
| Who | What to send | How |
|---|---|---|
| Most people | A flat edit | As now |
| Friends on Facebook | The 360 photo, exported as **360** | Insta360 app › photo › share › Facebook. This works from the Android app |
| Anyone on a phone | The 360 photo | Export as **360 photo** (not "reframed"). They can drag it round or tilt the phone in apps that support 360. **Test one in Google Photos** before relying on it |
| You, or a keen relative | Headset clips and 360 photos | Export as **360 video / 360 photo**, copy to the Meta headset like you did before |
| Anyone, anywhere | A 360 headset clip | Export as **360 video**, upload to YouTube. People drag it round on a phone or watch in a headset |

**The same 360 photo also gives you** a [tiny planet](../effects/tiny-planet-and-inverted.md)
and ordinary flat pictures pointed any way you like. One press, several
shots.
