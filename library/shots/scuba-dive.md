# Scuba dive (shore dive)

A dive that starts on the beach: you gear up by the car, walk down the sand,
wade in, swim out on the surface and go down. On Maui, typical spots are
Black Rock at Kāʻanapali, Ulua/Mokapu in Wailea and Mākena Landing, all
known for turtles. Your hands and attention belong to the dive, so the plan
is: **seal it up at the hotel, clip it on before the surf, press record at
the water's edge, and let it roll for the whole dive.** Long clips are the
point, because the turtle behind you is the one you'll only spot in the edit.

## Before you leave (the night before)
- [ ] **Charge the battery to 100%.** You can't swap it inside the dive
  case. One charge gives roughly 70 minutes of 5.7K30, which is enough for
  the wade-in, the surface swim and one dive.
- [ ] **Save a 5.7K30 preset to the Q/power button**, then switch on Dive
  Case Mode. Once the case is sealed, most settings are locked.
- [ ] **Seal the dive case at the hotel**, at a table, with clean dry hands.
  Check there's no hair, sand or salt on the O-ring. **Never open it on the
  beach.** One grain of sand on the seal is how cases flood.
- [ ] **Wipe both domes** inside and out. Put a small anti-fog insert in the
  case if you have one, because a warm car and cool water fog the dome.
- [ ] **Card space:** one dive is about 40–60 GB at 5.7K30. Start the day
  with at least 150 GB free.
- [ ] **A coiled lanyard with a clip** to attach the camera to a D-ring on your
  BCD. This matters more from shore than from a boat (see the entry below).

**Two dives in one day?** Swap the battery in the car between dives: towel off
the case, dry your hands, and open it with the case pointing down so drips
fall away from the inside. If that feels risky, film one dive and leave the
camera in the car for the other.

## Step by step
1. **Gear up at the car without recording.** It takes 15–20 minutes, and you
   need the battery for the water.
2. **At the water's edge, press record.** Clip the camera to your BCD on its
   lanyard, with the stick collapsed. The walk-in, the wade and the first
   look underwater make a good opening, and you won't touch the button again.
3. **Through the surf zone, keep both hands free.** Let the camera hang on
   its clip. Wait for a lull between waves, then walk in (shuffle your feet
   over sand, which also warns off any rays), put your fins on in
   chest-deep water as your buddy or guide shows you, and go. **Don't use the
   stick as a walking pole or hold it out in the waves.**
4. **Surface swim out:** once you're past the waves, unclip the camera and
   hold it out in front of you, **half in and half out of the water**. That
   gives you the beach behind and the reef below in one shot. See
   [Over-Under Split Shot](../effects/over-under-split-shot.md).
5. **Going down:** extend the stick once you're settled on the bottom.
6. **On the dive:** hold the camera **at arm's length, slightly ahead and
   above you**, with the case sideways so neither lens faces straight up at
   the surface. That's the "drone following a diver" look, and it shows
   you, your buddy and the reef at once. See
   [Underwater Third-Person Drone](../effects/underwater-third-person-drone-bts.md).
7. **Keep it still and turn your body, not your wrist.** Every twist moves
   the stitch line across whatever you're filming.
8. **Surge:** shore sites are shallow, and waves push you back and forth. In
   surge, shorten the stick and hold it close. A long stick in surge hits rocks
   and coral.
9. **Turtles:** they're the star of Maui's shore sites. Don't chase one. Stay
   put, keep about **3 m (10 ft)** away as Hawaii's wildlife guidelines ask,
   and let it pass the camera. Hold the camera slightly between you and it for
   the [Wildlife "Between" Selfie](../effects/wildlife-between-selfie.md). If
   one is sitting at a "cleaning station" (a rock where fish clean it), don't
   block its way up to breathe.
10. **Safety stop:** hover still with the stick out to the side for the three
    minutes. In shallow, bright water you'll get the reef, your buddy and
    sunbeams from the surface.
11. **Coming back in:** clip the camera back on **before you reach the
    waves**. Leave it recording. The surf exit is often the funniest bit,
    and you need both hands for it.

## While you're enjoying it
- **Dive first, film second.** Watch your air, your buddy and your
  direction back to shore.
- **Don't touch the reef or the bottom with the stick.**
- **Give a hand signal at good moments** (point at the thing, or a thumbs
  up to the camera). You can't say "mark that" underwater, but a signal is
  easy to spot when you scrub through.

## Optional extras (only if you feel like it)
- **Seabed set-down:** shore sites usually have plenty of sand, so this is
  easier than from a boat. On a sandy patch (never on coral), set the camera
  down for a minute while you and your buddy swim a slow circle round it.
  See [Seabed Drop and Swim-Around](../effects/seabed-drop-and-swim-around.md).
  Needs a weight or a Dive Buddy stand. Agree it with your buddy first, and
  don't do it in surge.
- **Lava arches** (for example the arches at Mākena / Five Caves): if you swim
  through one with the camera ahead, you get a natural transition. See
  [Swim-Through Doorway / Arch](../effects/swim-through-doorway-arch-transition.md).
  Only go through if your guide says it's safe.
- **Family on the beach:** if the family is waiting on the sand, the walk-in
  and walk-out clips catch them waving, so there's no need for a separate shot.

## Edit it fast (that evening)
1. **Copy the clip off the card first**, then edit.
2. Turn on **AquaVision**. If the reds look too strong, lower the intensity
   rather than turning it off.
3. Skim the timeline for your hand signals, and trim to the 3–5 best
   stretches. See [Finding moments in long clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md).
4. **Skip AI Edit / Auto Frame.** They lock onto the divers' faces (all mask)
   instead of the turtle.
5. Use [Deep Track](../editing/deep-track.md) on the turtle, drawing the box
   yourself. Use a fixed forward view for the rest.
6. Use the **walk-in** as the opening and the **surf exit** as the ending.

## Tips
- **Rinse the case in fresh water** after every dive while it's still
  closed (many beach parks have showers), then let it dry before opening.
- **Morning is usually calmest.** Wind and waves on Maui's west and south
  shores tend to pick up by midday. Check the surf forecast, and skip the
  dive if the entry looks rough.
- **Diving and altitude:** don't plan the Haleakalā summit (10,000 ft), or
  your flight home, for the day after diving. Ask your dive shop how long to
  wait. The usual guideline is at least 18–24 hours.
- Use reef-safe sunscreen. Hawaii bans some sunscreen ingredients.
