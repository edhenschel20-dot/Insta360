# Waterfalls and jungle trails

A walk along a rainforest path to a waterfall, for example the Pīpīwai Trail
through the bamboo forest to Waimoku Falls, or the short walks at Twin
Falls and Waikamoi on the Road to Hana. The plan: **chest-height walking
clip on the path, plant-and-go at the falls.**

## Before you leave (5 minutes)
- [ ] **Battery full plus a spare.** Pīpīwai is about 4 miles (6.5 km)
  return and 2–3 hours.
- [ ] **Wipe both lenses** and pack a dry microfibre cloth in a zip bag.
  Rainforest means drizzle and spray.
- [ ] **Stick with the mini tripod screwed on.** See
  [Plant-and-Go Stick Tripod](../effects/plant-and-go-stick-tripod.md).
- [ ] **Water for everyone.** In October 2026 the Kīpahulu (Pīpīwai) restrooms
  and water stations were closed for repairs, so check before you go.
- [ ] Wrist strap on. Paths are muddy and roots are slippery.

## Settings (set once)
| | |
|---|---|
| Walking under the canopy | **Video 5.7K30**, EV 0, white balance Auto (the forest is darker than it looks) |
| At the falls | Video 5.7K30, EV **−0.3 to −0.7** to keep the white water from blowing out |
| Dark bamboo forest | Still Video. The X5 copes. Switch to PureVideo only if it really looks murky on the preview |

See [Hiking & Views](hiking-and-views.md).

## On the path
- **Hold the stick at chest height, a little in front**, with the lenses
  pointing along the trail. The camera sees the path ahead and the family
  behind, and the seam stays on the trees at the sides.
- **Bamboo forest (Pīpīwai):** this is the star shot. Walk slowly along the
  boardwalk with the camera **low and pointed up**, so the bamboo closes in
  overhead into a tunnel. See [Look-Up Shot](../effects/look-up-shot.md). Stop
  for 20 seconds in the middle and just listen. The bamboo knocking together
  makes great audio.
- **Banyan tree (Pīpīwai, early on):** hold the camera up into the roots and
  branches for a [Rise Shot / Crane](../effects/rise-shot-crane.md), going
  slowly from ground level up to full stick height.
- **Bridges and streams:** hold the stick out over the rail (wrist strap on)
  for a "drone over the stream" look. See [Bridge Spinner](../effects/bridge-spinner.md)
  for the fancier version.
- Kids walking ahead: hold the camera at their height. See
  [Kid-Height Invisible Cinematographer](../effects/kid-height-invisible-cinematographer.md).

## At the waterfall
1. **Plant the stick** on a flat rock with the falls on one lens and the family
   on the other, and let it roll for 3–5 minutes while you all just look.
   That clip gives you the reveal, the reactions and a tiny planet.
2. **Reveal walk:** record the last 20 metres of the approach with the
   camera just behind your head, so you can swing from the path onto the
   falls in the edit. See [Up-and-Over Reveal](../effects/up-and-over-reveal.md).
3. **If swimming is allowed** (for example Twin Falls' lower pools), see
   [Waterfall Plunge-Pool](../effects/waterfall-plunge-pool.md). **Don't
   swim at Waimoku**, because rockfall closes the base. Check the posted signs
   at every falls, because flash floods are a real risk after rain upstream.

## While you're enjoying it
- Let it roll on the path. Say **"Mark that"** at the bamboo, the first view of
  the falls, or a gecko.
- If it's raining hard, put the camera away. Rain on a 360 lens ruins most
  of the frame.

## Edit it fast
1. Use a **fixed view along the path** for the walking parts.
2. **Bamboo tunnel:** keyframe a slow tilt from straight ahead up to the
   canopy. It's the best 10 seconds of the day. See [Keyframes with Easing](../editing/keyframes-with-easing.md).
3. **The falls:** use the planted clip. Use [Multi-View](../editing/multi-view.md)
   (falls plus faces) or a tiny planet with the falls in the middle.
4. Speed up the boring bits of path 4–8× rather than cutting them. It
   gives a sense of the journey.

## Tips
- **Spray:** rinse and shake the lens, then dab it with the dry cloth. Don't
  wipe with a finger.
- Stay on the path. Trails cross private and sacred land, and some Road to
  Hana "waterfalls" you'll see online are on private property with no-entry
  signs. Respect them.
- Drones are banned in Haleakalā National Park (which includes Pīpīwai).
  The stick "fake drone" is fine, and that's the point of it.
