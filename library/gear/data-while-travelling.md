# Handling footage while travelling (no laptop, no SSD)

How to keep a trip's footage safe and still share a few clips along the
way, with just the camera, its cards and an Android phone. The idea: **the
cards hold everything, the phone holds the best bits, and the big edit
waits until you're home.**

## Why not just put it all on the phone?
At 5.7K, an hour of footage can be up to about 80 GB (see
[Storage & batteries](storage-and-batteries.md)). One let-it-roll day can be
bigger than a phone's free space. The phone is for **picking and sharing**,
not for storing the whole trip.

## The rules
1. **Never wipe a card on the trip.** Until you're home, the card is the only
   copy of most of your footage.
2. **Change to a fresh card when one gets near full**, rather than running one
   card for a week. If the camera is lost, you only lose that card.
3. **Treat full cards like cash.** Keep them in a small card case, in the
   hotel safe or a different bag from the camera.
4. **Keep the best moments in two places:** on the card and on the phone.

## Making the cards last
- **TimeShift for driving.** It's roughly a tenth the size of normal video.
- **5.7K30, not 8K**, for everyday rolling.
- **Let it roll while something's happening**, but stop during true dead
  time: lunch, gearing up at the car, the walk back to the car park.
- If you're filming more than planned, **another card is the cheapest extra
  storage.** Use a genuine card from a proper retailer. See [Memory cards](memory-cards.md).

## Every evening (about 10 minutes)
- [ ] **Charge** all batteries and the power bank.
- [ ] In the Insta360 app, **connect to the camera and download only today's
  3–5 best clips** (your "mark that" moments). These are now your second copy
  of the moments that matter.
- [ ] **Optional:** make one quick 30–60 second clip and send it to family.
  Skip it if you're tired. Nothing is lost.
- [ ] Exported clips are small, so let **Google Photos back them up** on the
  hotel Wi-Fi. Full original files are too big for hotel Wi-Fi, so don't try.
- [ ] **Check the phone's free space.** If it's getting full, delete the
  downloaded copies you've already used. The originals are still on the cards.
- [ ] Note which card you're on, and put full cards away safely.

## Optional, about $10–15: a USB-C microSD reader
Plug it into the phone, and Android's **Files** app can copy whole clips
straight off a card onto the phone. It's faster than the camera's Wi-Fi.
It's worth it for one really important day (a turtle dive, for example)
when you want a full second copy of a particular clip.

## When you get home
1. **Copy every card to the computer** before doing anything else. Your
   home server is a good permanent home for the originals.
2. Only once they're copied, and you've checked the copies open, reuse the
   cards.
3. Do the big trip edit in **Insta360 Studio** on the computer. Scrubbing
   through hour-long clips is much quicker with a big screen and a mouse.
   See [Finding moments in long clips](../editing/finding-moments-in-long-clips.md).

## App or computer?
| | Phone app | Insta360 Studio (computer) |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Quick same-day clips, sharing | The full trip film, long clips |
| Why | Always with you, quick to share | Big screen and mouse, faster with long clips, easier keyframes |
| On a no-laptop trip | Yes | At home afterwards |

Both are free.

## What about Insta360's cloud (Insta360+)?
It's a paid subscription that can back up straight from the camera. On a
trip, hotel Wi-Fi is far too slow to upload 50+ GB a day, so it can't
replace the cards. It's fine for a few favourite clips if you already pay
for it. Prices vary by region, so check the Insta360 store.
