# Edit prompt: Parade

!!! info "Current version: 1 (2026-11-22), not tested yet"
    Built from the lessons of the fireworks tests. Use **Opus**. Paste it
    into a new chat in the Claude desktop app, with the clip open in
    Insta360 Studio. See [How to run it](how-to-run.md).

```text
Insta360 Studio is open with one 360 clip of a parade. Edit it into a
fun 16:9 clip with movement and creativity - not one fixed view for the
whole thing.

Keep EVERY float, band and performer that goes by - don't drop any to
make it shorter. Only cut the waiting and gaps between them. The length
is whatever is left once the dead time is gone.

If the clip still has edits from an earlier test, clear them all first
(keyframes, cuts, speed changes, effects) and confirm it's clean.

Camera moves to include (natural view, not fisheye):
- Opening: start on the street or the crowd waiting, then turn smoothly
  to the first float coming into view.
- Follow each float or group as it passes: pick it up as it arrives,
  pan with it as it goes by, then turn back for the next one. If
  Studio's Deep Track can lock onto a float, use it instead of hand
  keyframes.
- Keep the camera alive: slow push-ins (zoom in) on the best moments
  (dancers, characters waving, something spectacular), easing back out
  afterwards.
- If the kids react to something, glance at them for about 2 seconds,
  then back to the parade. At most two or three times in the whole clip.
- One dramatic moment for the best float - for example a quick zoom out
  to a wide or tiny-planet view for a second or two, then back in. This
  is the one place a curved look is welcome.
- End: the last float going away, then a slow pull-back or fade out.

Required - check each one before exporting:
1. REFRAME: keep the main subject well framed in 16:9 - never stuck at
   the edge - while still using the camera moves asked for above.
   Take a screenshot to confirm.
2. CUT: remove the dead time (see below).
3. TRANSITIONS AND EFFECTS: add a transition at the joins between cut
   sections, and at least one effect at the best moment. Choose ones
   that suit the clip.
In your report, confirm each of these 3 and say where you did it.

Editing style (always):
- Cut out dead time completely: setting up, waiting, people walking
  about, anything where nothing is happening. Don't speed those parts
  up - remove them. Keep a second or so before each key moment so the
  cuts aren't abrupt. Never cut the action itself: I want to see
  everything that happened, just without the waiting.
- Keep a normal, natural-looking view most of the time - no fisheye or
  heavy curving. Use a wide, curved look only once or twice as a
  deliberate effect, for a second or two.
- Focus on the action. A quick glance at people reacting is fine
  (under 2 seconds), then go straight back. Never stay on the person
  holding the camera.
- Smooth moves: ease in and out of every pan or tilt; no sudden jumps.
- Transitions and effects are welcome where they suit the moment, but
  don't overdo them.

Rules to keep this quick:
- Use keyframes for purposeful, slow moves - not one fixed view, but no
  jittery extra keyframes either.
- Check your work with no more than 3-4 screenshots.
- If something goes wrong twice, stop and tell me rather than retrying.
- Export at 1080p to my Downloads folder.
When you're done, tell me what you cut, the effects and transitions you
used, and roughly where they are.
```
