# Edit prompt: Carnival or fair (batch)

!!! info "Current version: 1 (2026-11-24), not tested yet"
    Works through **every clip in the folder** on its own. Use **Opus**.
    Before you start: load the clips into Studio, keep the laptop awake,
    and approve **Studio and File Explorer**. See
    [How to run it](how-to-run.md) and the overnight checklist on the
    [Lake day](lake-day.md) page.

Change the folder names and the "who to focus on" lines to suit the day.

```text
Work through this on your own: don't wait for me or ask me questions.
Make sensible choices yourself.

The job: there are several 360 clips from a day at a carnival in the
folder Videos\360unedited\Carnival. Edit EVERY clip in that folder,
one at a time, not just the one on screen. Export each one as its own
16:9 video.

How to work through them:
1. First, list every clip in the folder (name and length) in this chat.
2. Skip any clip that already has an export in
   Videos\360Claude\Carnival (so this prompt can be re-run to finish
   off).
3. Edit each clip as below, export it, then go to the next one.
4. After each clip, add one line to your running list here: clip name,
   done or skipped, and why if skipped.
5. If a clip fails twice at the same step, skip it, note why, and move
   on. Don't get stuck on one clip.
6. If you're close to a usage limit, finish the clip you're on and stop
   cleanly, with the list of what's done and what's left.

Who and what to focus on:
- THE TWO BOYS TOGETHER - my son and his friend. They are the main
  subject of the whole day. Find them in each clip and keep them in
  frame: walking around, queuing, on rides, reacting, laughing.
- RIDES: when the boys are on a ride, frame the ride nicely with them
  on it - show the ride moving, then push in on the boys.
- ROLLER COASTER - MUST CATCH: the boys are in the FRONT car. When the
  coaster comes round the corner towards the camera, have the view
  already pointing at that corner, then follow the front car and push
  in on the boys as it passes. This is the highlight of the day - give
  it time, and don't cut any of it.
- ME AND MY WIFE: if there's a good moment of the two of us together
  (smiling, laughing, reacting to the boys), include a few seconds of
  it. If there isn't a good one, skip it. Never show just the person
  holding the camera on their own, or the camera stick.
- Other people and the crowd: only a quick glance for atmosphere.

Keep all the action: every ride, every fun or funny moment with the
boys. Only cut the dead time (see below).

Camera moves to include (natural view, not fisheye):
- Opening of each clip: a wide view of the carnival (rides, lights,
  crowd), then ease in on the boys.
- Follow the boys as they move; if Studio's Deep Track can lock onto
  them, use it.
- Push in slowly on the best moments (the drop, the turn, big smiles,
  screams); ease back out afterwards.
- One special moment per clip, at most: a tiny-planet view of the
  carnival, or a quick zoom out to wide and back - for example as a
  ride spins. This is the one place a curved look is welcome.
- End of each clip: a slow pull-back to a wide view, or a fade out.

Required for EACH clip - check before exporting:
1. REFRAME: keep the subject (the boys, or the ride they're on) well
   framed in 16:9 - never stuck at the edge - while still using the
   moves above.
2. CUT: remove the dead time (see below).
3. CAMERA PASS: after cutting, go through every kept section one by
   one and aim the view at where the boys or the action are in THAT
   section - it moves around. Every section gets its own view and at
   least one move; never leave the whole clip pointing one way.
4. TRANSITIONS AND EFFECTS: a transition at the joins between cut
   sections, and at least one effect at the best moment.
5. EXPORT: 1080p, into Videos\360Claude\Carnival (create the folder if
   needed), named after the original clip with "-edit" on the end.

Editing style (always):
- Cut out dead time completely: long waits in a queue with nothing
  happening, walking between rides with nothing to see, the camera
  pointing at the ground or in a pocket, anything where nothing is
  happening. Don't speed those parts up - remove them. Keep a second
  or so before each key moment so the cuts aren't abrupt. Never cut
  the action itself: I want to see everything that happened, just
  without the waiting.
- Keep a normal, natural-looking view most of the time - no fisheye or
  heavy curving.
- Smooth moves: ease in and out of every pan or tilt; no sudden jumps.
- Transitions and effects are welcome where they suit the moment, but
  don't overdo them.

How Insta360 Studio behaves (learned in earlier runs - follow this):
- Permissions: at the very start, ask for access to BOTH Insta360
  Studio AND Windows File Explorer in one request. Without Explorer
  you can't bring Studio to the front, even when it's open.
- Clean start: reset the view angle to 0/0/0 and turn off all Imaging
  effects. Delete any old project segments that point at a missing
  file. Leave old saved versions in Clip Management alone.
- Find the action first: use the sound waveform and what's on screen to
  list every section to keep (with start and end times) BEFORE cutting.
- Make the cuts from the END of the clip backwards. Delete closes up
  the gap automatically, so cutting from the end keeps the earlier
  times on your list correct.
- Clicking a clip selects it and moves the playhead there.
- Ctrl+B splits at the MOUSE position if the mouse is over the
  timeline. Move the mouse off the timeline (e.g. onto the preview)
  before pressing Ctrl+B, so it splits at the playhead.
- Before pressing Delete, check the split really exists and only the
  gap is selected. If a split fails, Delete removes everything up to
  that point - Ctrl+Z straight away if that happens.
- Never touch, move or overwrite the original files.
- If Studio can queue exports, queue each one and move on to the next
  clip; otherwise wait for each export to finish.

Rules to keep this efficient:
- Use keyframes for purposeful, slow moves - not one fixed view, but no
  jittery extra keyframes either.
- Use as few screenshots as you need to check your work.
- Keep your notes in this chat short - one line per clip.

When everything is done, give me a short summary: each clip, how long
the edit is, the effects and transitions used, whether you found the
roller coaster corner and a good shot of me and my wife, and anything
skipped.
```
