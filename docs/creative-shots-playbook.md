# Creative Shots Playbook — Phone Portraits & X5 People Shots

Author: photography-expert agent (creative consultant pass). Date: 2026-09-26.
Companion to `docs/shot-recipes.md` (which covers "shoot for a fast edit" and
the settings side). This one is about **pro instincts that make a shot stand
out with little effort** — the kind of trick Ed already does when he drops to
the ground and shoots upward.

Labels: **[sourced]** = backed by a linked source in the list at the bottom;
**[mine]** = my own idea or judgement, not sourced. Insta360's own blog and
support pages blocked direct fetching today, so where I cite them I'm going on
their search-result summaries, not the full page — treat those as "consensus
from Insta360", and re-check the app if a mode name has moved.

## Part A — Phone / people photos (when someone hands Ed their phone)

The mindset: you have about ten seconds and one chance. Every trick below is
something you can do without changing any settings except tapping the screen
or switching 1x/0.5x. The order roughly follows what to do *in* those ten
seconds: pick the angle, pick the lens, put something in the foreground, fix
the light, get the person to move, then shoot more than once.

### A1. The low angle — and why Ed's trick works (and when it backfires) [sourced]
- **Do:** Crouch or put the phone near the ground, tilt up, subject filling the
  frame with sky/ceiling behind them. Get closer than feels natural.
- **Why it works:** Low angle makes the subject taller and more heroic, and the
  background turns into clean sky instead of a busy street. Foreground
  (ground, steps, grass) fills the bottom of the frame and gives depth.
- **When to avoid:** For a tight head-and-shoulders shot of one or two people,
  a low angle shoots up the nostrils and emphasises the chin/neck — most
  people are more flattered from eye level or *slightly above*. **Fix:** keep
  low angle for full-body shots (where it flatters legs and stance), and for
  head shots go to eye level or a touch higher. If someone asks for the low
  one anyway, tell them "chin down slightly, push your forehead toward me" —
  not "chin up", which makes it worse.

### A2. Full-body low angle: make the phone touch the ground [mine, builds on A1]
- **Do:** Actually rest the bottom edge of the phone on the ground/table/rail
  (phone upside down if that's the only way the lens is lowest). Use the
  volume button as shutter so you don't tilt it.
- **Why:** Half-way-down looks like an accident; all-the-way-down looks
  deliberate, and the surface itself becomes a mirror-like or leading
  foreground. Wet ground or a puddle is a free reflection.
- **Avoid:** Grimy floors on a borrowed phone; and cluttered ceilings indoors.

### A3. 0.5x ultrawide, but *close* [sourced]
- **Do:** Switch to 0.5x and step in until the subject is about 1 m away. Put
  them in the middle third, not at the edges.
- **Why:** Ultrawide exaggerates whatever is close and shrinks the background,
  so a person against a landmark gets that "epic scale" feel; low angle + 0.5x
  is the strongest combination for "look at this place" shots.
- **Avoid:** Faces at the frame edge (stretched heads/legs), and people closer
  than ~1 m for a head shot (big nose, small ears). Many phones also fix focus
  on 0.5x — don't expect a blurred background.

### A4. 0.5x from *above* for group shots [mine]
- **Do:** Hold the phone up high at 0.5x, tilt slightly down, everyone looks
  up.
- **Why:** Everyone's face is visible, nobody is hidden behind a shoulder, and
  the upward eyes look energetic. It's the safest group angle when there's no
  time to arrange people.
- **Avoid:** Too much tilt (foreheads big, feet tiny); keep the phone just
  above head height, not fully overhead.

### A5. "Tiny person, huge place" [sourced]
- **Do:** At 0.5x, put the person small in the frame, walking or standing at a
  point where the landscape/architecture is the subject.
- **Why:** Humans read scale from a person; the place looks bigger and the shot
  looks like a travel magazine.
- **Avoid:** When the person specifically wants *themselves* in the picture —
  do this as the second or third variation (see A15), not the only one.

### A6. Foreground framing: shoot *through* something [sourced]
- **Do:** Put a leaf, railing, doorway, arch, someone's shoulder, a glass, or a
  fence between you and the subject, very close to the lens, and tap the
  subject to focus.
- **Why:** The soft, out-of-focus foreground adds depth and looks like a lens
  effect with no Portrait Mode fakery. Ultrawide lets you get extremely close
  to the framing object and still keep the whole scene.
- **Avoid:** Foreground that covers the face; on 0.5x, foreground that's *too*
  sharp (then it's just clutter). Anything in the extreme corners of an
  ultrawide will be distorted.

### A7. Leading lines to the person [sourced/general craft]
- **Do:** Stand so a path, railing, shoreline, row of tables, or shadow runs
  from a bottom corner of the frame toward the subject. Low angle amplifies
  this — lines converge harder from the ground.
- **Why:** The eye follows the line straight to the person.
- **Avoid:** Lines that lead *past* the subject and out of frame; horizons cut
  through the neck.

### A8. Backlight / golden hour: sun behind the shoulder, tap the face [sourced]
- **Do:** Put the low sun just behind the subject's head or shoulder (not
  dead-centre behind the body). Tap the face on screen and, if it's dark, drag
  the exposure slider up a little.
- **Why:** Rim light on hair and shoulders separates them from the background
  and looks cinematic; phone HDR handles the rest.
- **Avoid:** Sun directly behind the torso (flare washes out the whole image,
  autofocus hunts). Midday overhead sun (raccoon-eye shadows) — turn them to
  face open shade instead.

### A9. Shade with a bright background [mine, standard craft]
- **Do:** In harsh sun, stand the subject just inside shade (under a tree,
  awning, doorway) facing out toward the light, with the sunny scene behind.
- **Why:** Soft, even face light with a glowing background — the same look as
  A8 but works at any hour.
- **Avoid:** Dappled tree shade with spots of sun on the face.

### A10. Window light indoors, phone at 45° [mine, standard craft]
- **Do:** Move the person next to a window, turn them 45° to it, shoot from the
  window side. Kill overhead lights if you can.
- **Why:** Cheapest "studio" light there is; the angle gives shape to the face.
- **Avoid:** Direct sun through the window on the face; the person sitting
  with the window *behind* them unless you're deliberately doing A8.

### A11. Portrait Mode: when it helps and when it lies [sourced]
- **Do:** Use Portrait Mode when the subject is still, well lit, and clearly
  in front of a background some distance away. Check the edges on the
  preview before handing the phone back.
- **Why:** It fakes a big-camera blurred background, which people love.
- **Avoid:** Frizzy or wispy hair, glasses, hats with brims, hands holding
  things, gaps between arm and body, a busy background right behind them,
  any movement, and low light — these are where the edge detection cuts hair
  off, blurs inside glasses, or draws halos. It also won't work on 0.5x and
  usually forces you 1–2 m away. If in doubt, take one normal and one
  portrait: the normal one is the safe one.

### A12. Get real blur instead: subject close, background far [sourced]
- **Do:** Step in close (1x or the 2x/3x tele if the phone has one), put lots
  of distance between the person and what's behind them.
- **Why:** This is how real lenses blur backgrounds. The tele lens also
  flattens features flatteringly — it's the "why do I look better in that
  one?" lens.
- **Avoid:** Tele in dim light (noisy / slow); ultrawide for close head shots.

### A13. The three-question posing kit [sourced]
Say these instead of "smile":
- "Walk toward me, don't look at me… now look at me." (walk-to-camera prompt)
- "Look at each other and tell them something embarrassing about yourself."
- "Look over your shoulder, then look at me on three."
- For one person: "Fix your hair / look down at your feet / now look up."
- **Why:** Prompts give people an action so they stop thinking about their
  face; movement kills the stiff pose.
- **Avoid:** Barking too many instructions; one prompt per shot.

### A14. Micro-posing that always helps [sourced/general craft]
- Shift weight onto the back foot, front knee soft.
- Hands doing something (pocket, bag strap, coffee, hair), not hanging.
- Chin slightly forward and down ("turtle out") — defines the jaw, works for
  everybody, and is the one thing that rescues a low angle.
- Groups: shoulders angled in toward the middle, not a police line-up;
  varying head heights (someone sits or leans).
- **Avoid:** Arms pressed against the body (widens them); everyone square to
  the camera.

### A15. The "take 3 variations" habit [mine, common pro practice]
Every time: **(1)** the shot they asked for, straight, eye level; **(2)** a
low-angle or 0.5x version (Ed's trick); **(3)** a tighter one, or a candid
after they think you're done. Give the phone back saying "I took a few, pick
your favourite." Why: the *second* choice is usually the one they post, and it
costs 10 seconds.

### A16. Burst for candids and jumps; Live Photo as a safety net [sourced]
- **Do (iPhone):** Tap the face to lock focus, then slide the shutter button
  left to burst (up to ~10 fps). Start before the action and hold through it.
  On Android, hold the shutter or check for a "burst" gesture. Live Photo
  (1.5 s either side) is the fallback for blinks — they can pick a key frame
  later.
- **Why:** Jumps, laughs, hair flips, kids and dogs are all timing; burst
  turns timing into choosing.
- **Avoid:** Burst and Live Photo can't run together on iPhone; burst in dim
  light (blur). And don't leave 60 burst frames on someone's phone — say "keep
  the best and delete the rest".

### A17. The "after" shot [mine]
Keep the phone up and take one more the moment they relax after "got it".
Genuinely often the best frame. Costs nothing.

### A18. Clean the lens [sourced/general]
Wipe the camera on a shirt before the first frame. A greasy lens is the #1
cause of the hazy "why does my phone look worse than yours" photo, and it
ruins backlit shots (A8) completely.

### A19. Tap-to-focus-and-hold, then reframe [sourced]
- **Do:** Press and hold on the face until AE/AF Lock appears, then recompose.
- **Why:** Lets you put the person off-centre (rule of thirds) or expose for a
  face against a bright sky without the phone re-deciding.
- **Avoid:** Forgetting it's locked when you point somewhere else.

### A20. Horizon and verticals [general craft]
Straight horizon unless you are *very* obviously tilting on purpose. On
ultrawide, keep the phone level (not tilted up) if there are buildings, or
they'll lean inward; tilt up only when the lean is the point (A1).

### A21. Reflections and mirrors [mine]
Puddle, shop window, sunglasses, a wet table, a phone screen — get the lens
right down to the reflective surface (A2) and shoot the person and their
reflection. Instant "how did you do that" with zero editing.

### A22. Silhouette as the bonus frame [mine]
At sunset, tap the *sky* instead of the face and the person goes to
silhouette. Take it as variation 3 (A15) — profiles and separated limbs
read best; a blob of people does not.

## Part B — Insta360 X5 creative shots of people and moments

Two principles that underpin most of these (both **[sourced]**):
1. **Camera height is the shot.** Viewers of reframed 360 on a phone see the
   world from the camera's height. At 30 cm off the ground, everyone is a
   giant; at 3 m up, everyone is a figure in a landscape. Eye level is the
   *least* interesting height for 360 — 360 Rumors argues for waist level (or
   a 30° downward slope to the subject) for normal shots, precisely because
   eye level puts heads dead-centre with a squashed body.
2. **The stick is invisible, so the stick is the crane, the dolly, the
   tripod and the drone.** Every "how did you get that?" shot below is just
   the stick held somewhere a human hand can't normally be.

Reframing notes: "reframe" below means the Insta360 app's reframe view (drag
to point the virtual camera, pinch for FOV, keyframes for movement). Effects
named in **bold** are one-tap in the app / Shot Lab as of 2026, but the list
changes; check the app.

### B1. The giant shot (low camera, subject looming) [sourced + mine]
- **Setup:** Stick at 20–40 cm off the ground (or the X5 on its own mini
  tripod on the ground, timer 3–5 s), lens pointed at the person 1–2 m away.
  Ask them to step in and look down at it, or stride *over* it.
- **Edit:** Photo: reframe with FOV wide, tilt up to the face, keep a bit of
  ground. Video: 2 keyframes — start looking at feet, end on face — or just
  let them walk over and hold the view.
- **Wow:** Ed's phone trick, but the camera is far lower than any phone can
  go and ultrawide beyond any phone lens; feet and hands are huge, head small,
  sky behind. Best full-body; same chin caveat as A1 for tight faces.

### B2. Camera on the ground, everyone leans in [sourced + mine]
- **Setup:** X5 flat on the ground (or table) on its mini tripod, timer 5 s
  or remote/app shutter, group stands in a ring around it and leans over, heads
  meeting above the lens. Photo mode, 72 MP, HDR photo if bright sky.
- **Edit:** Reframe straight up (fisheye/wide FOV) for the classic "circle of
  faces" look, *or* tap **Tiny Planet** for a ring of people standing on a
  ball.
- **Wow:** One capture, two completely different portraits; nobody had to
  hold a camera and everybody is in it — including Ed.

### B3. Tiny planet group portrait (standing) [sourced]
- **Setup:** Stick planted vertical, camera 1–1.5 m up, group in a loose
  circle 2–3 m out, all facing the camera. Open sky above, something
  patterned on the ground (tiles, grass, sand, a plaza) — the ground becomes
  the planet, so it's the most important part. Keep the horizon level.
- **Edit:** One-tap Tiny Planet in the app; drag to rotate which person is
  "on top"; zoom in a touch so the planet fills the frame.
- **Wow:** The definitive 360 group photo. The forum trick "Tiny Planet
  Selfie" is the same idea with Ed alone holding the stick straight up above
  his head, so he stands on the pole of the planet.

### B4. Jump Planet / any Shot Lab jump template [sourced]
- **Setup:** Shot Lab → Jump Planet (or the current equivalent). Camera on
  extended invisible stick, everyone jumps on cue; the app uses the jump as the
  trigger for a tiny-planet transition.
- **Edit:** Automatic — that's the point. Follow the on-screen recording
  guide.
- **Wow:** Looks heavily edited, took 15 seconds. Shot Lab has 40+ templates
  (Sky Swap, AI Warp, Clone Trail, Nose Mode, Fly Lapse and others); worth
  five minutes scrolling it before a trip so Ed knows which ones exist.

### B5. The overhead invisible-stick "drone" portrait [sourced]
- **Setup:** Stick fully extended (1.2 m standard; 3 m with the Extended
  Edition) straight up and slightly forward, Ed and friends below. Photo mode
  timer, or video and grab a frame.
- **Edit:** Reframe looking straight down, FOV wide — people as a top-down
  cluster on the ground pattern. Or angle 45° down for the "drone at 3 m"
  look.
- **Wow:** Everyone assumes a drone. The 3 m stick is the single accessory
  that most changes what Ed can do with people shots.

### B6. Stick as crane: the rise-and-reveal [sourced (forum "Dive Shot" reversed) + mine]
- **Setup:** Start with the camera at knee height in front of the person, then
  smoothly raise the stick overhead over ~3 s while stepping back half a
  pace. Do it once, hold still before and after.
- **Edit:** Keyframe 1 on the face at the start, keyframe 2 looking down at
  the person + surroundings at the top. Horizon Lock on.
- **Wow:** A jib/crane move that reveals the location behind the person. The
  reverse (start high, dive down to the face) is the forum's "Dive Shot" and
  works as an intro.

### B7. Stick as dolly: the walk-past / orbit [sourced (forum "360 Orbit") + mine]
- **Setup:** Person stands still. Ed walks a slow half-circle around them at
  2–3 m with the stick at waist height, camera lens facing them.
- **Edit:** Deep Track on the person (they're the only subject; Ed is behind
  the camera axis, so the AI shouldn't grab him). Optional slow-mo.
- **Wow:** A gimbal-style orbit with the background sweeping behind them.
  Waist height, not eye level, so the proportions look right.

### B8. Bullet time without throwing: swing, spin, or lean [sourced]
- **Setup:** Bullet Time mode (5.7K120 for slow-mo). Options that never leave
  the hand: (a) the classic overhead swing on the Bullet Time handle/stick,
  ~1 s per rotation, one lens up, one down; (b) Ed holds the stick out
  horizontally and *spins on the spot* — the camera orbits him; (c) for a
  *friend*: they stand still, Ed swings the camera around them in a wide
  overhead arc so they're the orbited subject.
- **Edit:** Bullet Time auto-reframes; trim only. For (c), reframe to keep
  the friend centred.
- **Wow:** The Matrix orbit, no throwing, no risk to the lenses. Good light
  only (low light = blur at 120 fps).

### B9. The "nose mode" / camera-on-face selfie [sourced]
- **Setup:** Shot Lab **Nose Mode** (or handheld with the camera right at
  face level, 30 cm away, in Single-Lens/Me Mode).
- **Edit:** Template does it.
- **Wow:** Exaggerated, funny, and people always ask to do it too.

### B10. Between-lenses "two worlds" split [sourced]
- **Setup:** Stick planted between two people/things — the friend facing one
  lens, the view/other friend facing the other.
- **Edit:** Multi-View / split screen template; no tracking needed.
- **Wow:** Reaction + the thing they're reacting to in one frame (recipe 14 in
  `shot-recipes.md` covers the interview version).

### B11. Doorway / tunnel reveal with a 360 roll [sourced (forum "360 Roll", "Tilt")]
- **Setup:** Walk through a doorway, gate, or trees with the stick overhead,
  person walking ahead of Ed. Steady pace, one direction.
- **Edit:** Keyframe looking straight up at the doorframe as you pass, then
  tilt down to the person and the view opening beyond.
- **Wow:** The ceiling-to-vista tilt is the 360 signature transition and it's
  just two keyframes.

### B12. Through-the-legs / under the table [mine]
- **Setup:** X5 on the ground on its tripod between or beside people's feet
  (dinner table, picnic, kids playing), record 20–30 s stationary.
- **Edit:** Deep Track on whoever is talking / reframe upward through the
  table edge or knees.
- **Wow:** A viewpoint nobody normally sees — mouse's-eye view of a family
  dinner. Also the least intrusive way to film people naturally (recipe 7
  logic).

### B13. Handshake / hug from *inside* the moment [mine]
- **Setup:** Stick short (30 cm), camera held between two people as they hug,
  high-five or clink glasses.
- **Edit:** Reframe wide FOV, tiny bit of fisheye, centred on the hands/faces.
- **Wow:** The camera is inside the action rather than watching it; a phone
  can't get there without blocking the moment.

### B14. Tiny planet *inverse* (rabbit hole / tunnel) [sourced (forum "Inverted Tiny Planet")]
- **Setup:** Same as B3.
- **Edit:** Tiny Planet, then rotate the view to invert it (or use the
  Inverted Tiny Planet option) — the sky becomes a ball and the group stands
  around a hole.
- **Wow:** Second output from the same capture; some groups look better this
  way when the ground is boring and the sky is dramatic.

### B15. Enter-the-planet opening [sourced (forum "Enter Planet Shot")]
- **Setup:** Any tiny-planet capture with the group.
- **Edit:** Keyframe 1 tiny planet zoomed out; keyframe 2 normal view on the
  faces. The app interpolates the "fly in".
- **Wow:** A 4-second title card for any group video.

### B16. Camera on a table, Ed walks away: the third-person "leave" [mine]
- **Setup:** Plant the camera on a table/rock at waist height, start
  recording, walk away with a friend into the scene, then keep walking.
- **Edit:** Deep Track on the two of you; or fixed reframe.
- **Wow:** A locked-off "cinema" wide with nobody holding anything. Fixes the
  "I'm always in the shot holding the stick" problem by making Ed a subject
  without the stick being the point.

### B17. Ski-pole / bike / backpack third-person rig [sourced]
- **Setup:** Stick clamped to a ski pole, backpack strap, or bike (forum "3rd
  Person View"; Insta360 tips mention the ski-pole clip). Camera 1 m+ behind
  and above Ed.
- **Edit:** Fixed forward reframe or Deep Track on the person ahead.
- **Wow:** Video-game follow cam with no hands. Nothing to reframe if Ed
  decides the front lens is the one facing forward.

### B18. Low crane along the ground (the "slide") [mine]
- **Setup:** Stick horizontal, camera 10 cm off the ground, Ed walks past a
  seated or lying-down person (beach, grass, café step).
- **Edit:** Deep Track or a single sideways-looking reframe.
- **Wow:** Slider shot at ant height, huge foreground texture rushing past.

### B19. Dolly zoom on a friend [sourced (forum "Vertigo/Dolly Zoom")]
- **Setup:** Walk toward the person at steady speed, ~3 m to 1 m, camera at
  their chest height.
- **Edit:** Keyframe FOV wide at the start and narrower at the end (or the
  reverse) so the background lurches while they stay the same size.
- **Wow:** The Hitchcock effect; 360 makes it trivial because FOV is a
  slider, not a lens.

### B20. Clone Trail / "same person three times" [sourced]
- **Setup:** Shot Lab Clone Trail (or the current name): camera static, the
  friend moves through several positions; the app composites the copies.
- **Edit:** Automatic.
- **Wow:** The photo everyone wants to try next; static camera means Ed can
  step out of shot.

### B21. Sky Swap for the flat grey day [sourced]
- **Setup:** Any tiny-planet or overhead group shot with a boring sky.
- **Edit:** Shot Lab Sky Swap.
- **Wow:** Fixes the one thing that kills 360 stills — a featureless sky
  covering half the sphere. Use sparingly; it looks like what it is if
  overdone.

### B22. Time Freeze portrait: a still with a moving camera [sourced (forum "Time Freeze")]
- **Setup:** One 72 MP photo of a person/group, ideally mid-gesture (arms
  out, mid-laugh).
- **Edit:** In the app, animate the reframe on the *photo* (pan, tilt, zoom
  keyframes) and export as video.
- **Wow:** A frozen moment the camera flies around — pairs with B15 as an
  intro, and it's a photo, so no motion blur issues.

### B23. The "how did you get that?" self-portrait [sourced (Tiny Planet Selfie) + mine]
- **Setup:** Stick vertical straight above Ed's head, fully extended, Ed
  looking up, in a spot with a patterned ground. Photo timer.
- **Edit:** Tiny Planet — Ed appears standing on the top of his own planet
  with the whole location wrapped around him. Alternative: reframe straight
  down at max FOV for the "floating drone selfie".
- **Wow:** No visible stick, no drone, no helper. This is the one that gets
  asked about most.

## Ed's top 10 (best wow per minute of effort)

1. **A1/A2 refined** — keep the low angle for full body, go eye-level-or-above
   for tight faces, always say "chin forward and down". Zero effort, immediate
   improvement on the trick Ed already uses.
2. **A15 take three** — asked-for shot, low/0.5x shot, candid "after" shot.
3. **A8 backlight + tap the face** — the single biggest jump in "does this look
   professional" for phone portraits.
4. **A6 shoot through something** — foreground blur without Portrait Mode.
5. **A13 one posing prompt** — "walk toward me, now look at me".
6. **B2 camera on the ground, everyone leans in** — one capture, two portraits
   (ring of faces + tiny planet), Ed is in it.
7. **B23 tiny planet self-portrait** — the "how did you get that?" shot.
8. **B1 the giant** — 360 version of the low angle, further down than a phone
   can go.
9. **B5 3 m overhead drone shot** — buy the extended stick; most people
   can't tell it from a drone.
10. **B8 hand-held bullet time (spin on the spot, or orbit a friend)** — the
    ready-to-share clip with no throwing.

Honourable mentions: B6 crane reveal (two keyframes, looks like a jib), B4
Shot Lab jump templates (zero editing), A16 burst for jumps.
